# ADR-0047: Several outputs per canvas: record and stream at once, encoders shared when their settings match

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-11-04
- Deciders: Salih (owner)
- Amends: [ADR-0041](0041-studio-recording-on-a-running-engine.md) decision 3 ("one recording at a time")

## Context

The desktop app streams (PR #125) and records (PR #96), but only one at a time: `Studio` holds a single attached output (`AlreadyRecording`), and the app refuses Record while live and Go live while recording. Recording a local copy of a live stream is one of the most common uses of a broadcasting tool, and the owner's roadmap lists it as the next step after the Go live button.

What exists:

- `Studio::start_recording(adapters, out)` attaches one chain to the running graph: a video export (render partition, GPU to system memory readback, the P4 exception of ADR-0038), a video encoder (`partitions.video_encoder`), an audio encoder (`partitions.audio_encoder`) and an output (`partitions.output`). `stop_recording` removes the chain with `Engine::apply_draining`, which runs final ticks only for partitions that end up empty.
- The graph allows one output pin to feed several inputs. Every link that crosses partitions gets its own queue: a video mailbox that shares the frame (`VideoFrame::share`, a reference count, no copy), a packet channel per link, a set of audio buffers per link. The studio already fans out: the compositor's `canvas` feeds the preview and the export; the mixer's `main` feeds the meter sink and the audio encoder.
- A packet queue endpoint that starts begins at a keyframe (media graph spec), so a consumer that joins a running encoder never gets a broken stream.
- A partition has a worker only while its schedule has nodes (ADR-0027), so partitions can be added at studio start at no cost (P13).
- The export's readback hands out frames from a fixed set of `DOWNLOAD_BUFFERS` (6) system-memory buffers, sized for one consumer chain (render slot, queue, encoder input).

Forces:

- Cost (P4, P13): encoding is the dominant cost of an output; the readback is next. A second output should pay for a second encoder only when it needs different settings, and nothing for an output that is not running.
- Settings (owner, 2026-11-03: every quality parameter is a user setting): recordings and streams usually differ (a stream at 6 Mbit/s CBR with a 2 s keyframe interval, a recording at a higher rate or constant quality), so sharing must not be forced.
- Isolation: a network transport that stalls or reconnects must never block or drop the file being written; stopping the recording must not interrupt the stream, and the reverse.
- Hot paths (P15): node `process` code, queues and the readback are hot paths; this decision should not need to change them.

## Decision

1. **A studio runs up to `MAX_OUTPUTS` (2) outputs at once, each with an `OutputId`.** `Studio::start_output(adapters, out) -> Result<OutputId, RecordError>` replaces `start_recording`; `stop_output(id)` replaces `stop_recording`; `outputs()`, `output(id)` (its `StartReport`), `output_failure(id)`, `link(id)` and `output_progress(id)` replace the single-output accessors; `progress()` keeps the source and worker counters only. A third output fails with `RecordError::TooManyOutputs` before anything is chosen or opened. `Recording` (one output, `rbs-cli`) keeps its contract.
2. **Each output has its own output partition**, so a slow or reconnecting transport stalls only its own queue. `Partitions` gains `second: OutputPartitions { video_encoder, audio_encoder, output }` (defaults 5, 6, 7) next to the existing ids, which form the first slot. The studio adds every slot's partitions at start; they get a worker only while something is scheduled on them. Partitions are allocated **by role, separately**: an output takes the first free output partition, a new video chain the first free video encoder partition, a new audio encoder the first free audio encoder partition. A chain is held by every output that uses it and removed with the last one, so it may stay in the first slot's partitions after the output that opened it stopped. With `MAX_OUTPUTS` = 2 and at most one chain of each kind per output, a free partition of each role exists whenever fewer than two outputs run. A partition with an instance whose take is still pending (its swap or drain in flight) is not free: a stop followed at once by a start must not cancel the stopped output's pending drain (engine: a part that gives the partition nodes again cancels it).
3. **Encoders are shared when the settings match, never otherwise.** A new output reuses a running video encoder when its `VideoSettings` equal those the encoder was opened with and that encoder's adapter is a candidate of the new output's selection (the output's registry holds it and it meets the codec, memory and pixel-format requirement; a registry narrowed to another encoder, a user's explicit choice, gets its own). The audio encoder is reused when the `AudioSettings` are equal and the same rule holds. A shared encoder's `packets` pin feeds both outputs; each link has its own packet queue. Otherwise the output gets its own chain in a free slot's encoder partitions. Video and audio are decided independently.
4. **One export per video encoder.** A video encoder chain is its export plus its encoder; a second encoder gets a second export on the render partition, fed from the same compositor `canvas` (texture shared, no copy). The readback and its buffer count stay as they are; sharing one export between two encoders needs more buffers in `rbs-gpu` (a hot-path change with a memory cost) and is left to a follow-up decided with measurements.
5. **Joining a running encoder starts at its next keyframe.** The new output's queues begin at a keyframe (the existing endpoint rule), so a recording started while streaming with shared settings starts up to one keyframe interval late; its audio before the first video packet is held and trimmed by the output's aligned start (ADR-0043; the hold, `START_HOLD` = 256 packets, about 5 s of AAC, drops its oldest when a keyframe interval is longer, which is harmless). An output stopped before its first keyframe arrives has no video and possibly no media at all. The encoder is not asked for an early keyframe: that needs a parameter channel on the encoder node, a hot-path change, and would put an unplanned keyframe into the other output's stream. Follow-up if the delay matters in practice.
6. **A shared encoder serves its outputs alike.** It takes no request that would change one output's stream only (an early keyframe for one output, a per-output bitrate change, a transport's keyframe request); a future feature that needs one ends the sharing first (the output gets its own chain). The encoder's own reaction to queue overflow (ADR-0026) changes both streams and is accepted.
7. **Stopping an output drains what only it uses.** `stop_output` removes the output node, and every encoder chain no other running output uses, then applies with `apply_draining`: the partitions this empties run their final ticks in pipeline order (ADR-0041 decision 4), so the stopped output delivers its queue and writes its trailer; partitions still used by the other output keep running untouched. Frames inside a shared encoder at that moment do not reach the stopped output (at most the encoder's latency, a few frames). The other output is not touched.
8. **Counters and failures per output.** An output's `Progress` counts from its start request: when it joins a running encoder chain, the chain's counters at that moment are its base and are subtracted. Up to one GOP encoded before the joining output's first keyframe is in its count; the packets its queue dropped while waiting for that keyframe are the queue's `resync_drops` and are not shown as loss. A permanent failure of an output node fails that output only; a permanent failure of an encoder chain node fails every output that uses it. The composition root stops a failed output with `stop_output`.
9. **The desktop app records and streams independently.** Record and Go live no longer exclude each other; each keeps its own `OutputId`, state, summary and failure handling; quitting stops both (the stream still asks first); the canvas cannot change while either runs. The control API's `record.*` methods are unchanged and refer to the recording output only.

## Consequences

- Record and stream at once costs, with different settings, one more export (a GPU conversion and readback per frame), one more encoder session and three more worker threads (two encoders and an output); with matching settings one output worker only. Nothing runs for an output slot that is not used.
- No hot path code changes: nodes, queues and the readback are unchanged; the work is graph composition on the control thread. Packet fan-out (a listed hot path) is used more, not changed. P15 applies only if `queue.rs`, `schedule.rs` or a node's `process` is touched. Acceptance: CPU of the process and the render and encoder ticks are measured on hardware for stream alone, record plus stream with different settings and with equal settings, and recorded in the PR.
- A hardware encoder with a session limit may refuse the second session; selection then moves to the next candidate (another hardware encoder or a software one), as for any refused open.
- Semver: breaking for `Studio` (pre-1.0; callers in `rbs-record`, `rbs-app` and the tests are updated in the same change): `start_recording`, `stop_recording`, `is_recording`, `recording`, `recording_failure` and the argument-less `link` are replaced, `shutdown` returns every output's report, and `RecordError::AlreadyRecording` is removed (removing a variant breaks exhaustive matches even of a `#[non_exhaustive]` enum within the workspace and any `matches!` on it). Additive: `RecordError::TooManyOutputs`, `Partitions::second`, `OutputPartitions`, `StartReport::{video_shared, audio_shared}`, `MAX_OUTPUTS`, `OutputId`. `Recording` is unchanged.
- A recording that joins a shared encoder starts up to a keyframe interval after it was asked for; the UI says "Starting the recording…" until it does.
- Tests (ADR-0032: `TestClock`, waits on packet counts and tick indices, properties rather than exact frames): two outputs with different settings each get their own encoders and complete files; two with equal settings share the encoders (no second session, packets in both outputs, the joining output's first video packet a keyframe no older than the join less what the encoder held); stopping either leaves the other running and delivering, its encoder partitions stopped only when unused; a new output after a stop takes the free partitions; a third output is refused; an output that cannot start leaves the running one alone; per-output counters count from the start. In `rbs-graph`, a consumer link added mid-GOP to a running packet producer starts at a keyframe, the first consumer sees no gap, and the ticks after the swap allocate nothing.
- Docs: the recording spec's Studio section and contract, the open question on several outputs.

## Alternatives considered

- **Always separate chains** (two exports, two encoders, even with equal settings): simplest, but doubles the encoding cost in the case where nothing differs. Rejected as the only mode; it is what different settings get.
- **Always one shared encoder** (OBS's "same as stream"): forces the stream's bitrate and keyframe interval on the recording, against the owner's rule that quality settings are the user's. Rejected.
- **One export for all encoders:** saves a readback when settings differ, but the readback's fixed buffer set would have to grow for every output a studio might start (memory paid while one output runs, a hot-path change in `rbs-gpu`). Deferred to a measured follow-up (decision 4).
- **Force a keyframe when an output joins:** starts the joining output at once, but adds an encoder parameter (hot path) and an unplanned keyframe in the running stream. Deferred (decision 5).
- **A second engine per output:** would capture every device twice (an exclusive camera cannot) and the two would not show the same frames. Rejected (as in ADR-0041).
- **Any number of outputs:** partitions are added at start, so a fixed small slot count keeps that free; two covers record plus stream. Raising `MAX_OUTPUTS` later is additive.

## Review

- 2026-11-04, independent AI session (ADR-0014, `architecture-review` skill; got the proposal, the specs and the code only). Verdict: changes requested, no blockers. It confirmed against the code: an output pin feeds several links, each crossing its own queue, and adding one to a running producer only adds a writer to its partition (the producer stays prepared); a new packet writer starts in resync mode, and the muxers set their own origin, so mid-timeline starts are as today; `apply_draining` with only the output partition emptied drains it while the shared encoder partition swaps plainly; `add_partition` starts no thread. Findings and what changed:
  1. (major) Slots tied output and encoder partitions together, so a chain left behind by a stopped output could block a third start. Fixed: partitions are allocated by role (decision 2, with the invariant); test: a new stream after the first output stopped takes the free output partition and shares the remaining chain.
  2. (major) A partition could be reused while its drain was pending after a `SwapTimeout`. Fixed: a partition with a pending take is not free (decision 2).
  3. (major) No graph-level test of a consumer joining a running producer. Added: `a_consumer_added_to_a_running_producer_starts_at_a_keyframe` (keyframe start, no gap for the first consumer, no allocation per tick after the swap).
  4. (minor) The counter base is taken at the request, media starts at the keyframe. Resolved by definition: counts are "since requested", the resync drops are the queue's (decision 8, spec).
  5. (minor) The aligned-start hold and an output stopped before its first keyframe. Stated in decision 5 and the spec.
  6. (minor) A shared encoder links the outputs' futures. Added decision 6.
  7. (minor) P15 and P4: measurement as an acceptance criterion (Consequences); no hot-path code is touched.
  8. (minor) Semver. Stated in Consequences, including the removed `AlreadyRecording`.
  9. (minor) Test method. Stated in Consequences.
  10. (process) Code was written while the ADR was Proposed. The ADR is updated before the change merges; the owner's ADR policy lets the implementing thread accept it after the review is resolved.
