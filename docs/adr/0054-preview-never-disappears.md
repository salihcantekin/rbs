# ADR-0054: The preview picture never disappears behind popups

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-11-06
- Deciders: Salih (owner)
- Builds on: ADR-0034 (macOS preview layer), ADR-0036 (Windows preview layer), ADR-0048 (selection marks on the layer), ADR-0013 (zero cost when unused)
- Supersedes in part: ADR-0034 decision 4 (hide the layer while toolkit content overlaps it) and the same rule for Windows in ADR-0036 and ADR-0048 decision 1 ("hidden and shown with the layer")

## Context

The preview and program pictures are native layers over their panel: a Core Animation layer on macOS (ADR-0034) and a DirectComposition visual in a topmost composition target on Windows (ADR-0036). They sit above everything Slint draws in the window. Slint 1.18's winit backend draws a `PopupWindow` inside its window (`i-slint-backend-winit` `winitwindowadapter.rs`: "We don't render popups as separate windows yet"; `i-slint-core` `window.rs` places it as a `PopupWindowLocation::ChildWindow`, clamped into the window by `popup::place_popup`).

So that in-window popups, toasts and the error bar are not drawn under the picture, the app hid the whole layer while any of them was open: each popup added itself to a counter (`Studio.overlays`), whether or not it overlapped the picture. The owner tested the source shapes work on macOS (2026-11-06): opening the colour picker in Properties blanked the preview although the picker was beside it, and the same had happened with other menus. The owner's rule: the central picture must never disappear when a popup, dialog or window opens.

The owner approved two steps (2026-11-06). Step 1, done without a new decision because it is what ADR-0034 decision 4 already says: the layer hides only while a cover overlaps what it draws (the picture or a selection mark). The covers are every open in-window `PopupWindow`, read from Slint itself (`rbs_app::popups`, `i_slint_core::window::WindowInner::active_popups`, exact-version dependency, checked by `tests/popup_covers.rs`), and the toasts, which report their rectangles in `Shell`; they are read before every redraw of the main window (`rbs_ui::cover`). Step 2, this ADR: what to do when a cover does overlap the picture, chosen by cost between splitting the picture around the cover and drawing popups as separate OS windows.

## Decision

1. **Rule.** While its panel is visible, the preview or program picture is never hidden by something the user opens: popups, pickers, menus, dialogs, toasts, the error bar or other windows. At most the part that something actually covers is not shown. Stated exceptions: a cover that is not finite hides the picture (decision 3), more covers than the tile cap leave small uncovered pieces out (decision 3), and Windows until its hardware check (decision 5). This joins U21 ("Always reachable, never in the way") as a bullet.
2. **Covers as in step 1.** Every open in-window `PopupWindow` (read from Slint, so a new popup needs no code of its own) and the toasts, the bottom toast and the error bar at the top, which report their rectangles in `Shell` (`bottom-toast`, `top-toast`), read before every redraw of the main window. A full-window modal scrim drawn inside the window would cover the whole picture; RBS dialogs are OS windows (decision 6), and an in-window modal needs its own decision.
3. **One placement, one region function.** `rbs_gpu::share::geometry`, platform-neutral and tested on every platform, gives both what the layer draws and what stays visible:
   - The picture's rectangle is `fit(panel rectangle, surface size)` with its edges snapped to device pixels (`snap`; macOS takes the scale from the superlayer's `contentsScale`, as the marks already do; Windows works in physical pixels). Both layers place the picture with this rectangle; macOS no longer relies on `kCAGravityResizeAspect` for it. The layer computes the region itself from the covers it is given (`set_cutouts`); the shell only passes covers. Where the shell needs the picture's rectangle (step 1's overlap test, pointer mapping for marks), it fits the same surface size, not the canvas size, whose aspect can differ by the surface's rounding.
   - Covers are snapped outward (start floored, end ceiled to device pixels), so no part of a popup's edge stays under the picture. A popup drawing outside its geometry (a drop shadow) is not covered; popups keep such effects inside their geometry (none has a shadow today).
   - A cover that is not finite hides the layer (`set_hidden`) instead of cutting, as step 1 does.
   - The picture region is that rectangle minus the union of the covers, split into horizontal bands at every cover edge; each band's uncovered x-intervals are pieces, and a piece equal to the one above it in x merges with it. Every edge is a snapped edge of the picture or of a cover, so neighbouring tiles share exact device-pixel edges (no seams).
   - The region is exact for up to five covers that overlap the picture (popup, submenu, a `Select` list in it, toast and error bar): k covers give at most 2k + 1 bands of at most k + 1 intervals, 11 x 6 = 66 pieces for five, under the cap of 72 tiles. With more covers, beyond the cap the smallest pieces are left out: a known limit where rule 1 holds only approximately.
   - The arithmetic uses scratch of fixed capacity kept by the layer: no allocation per redraw.
   - Selection marks (ADR-0048) may extend into the letterbox bars, so they are clipped to the panel's rectangle minus the covers, not to the picture region; at most 128 mark pieces, beyond that the smallest are left out (marks are decoration of the picture).
4. **A cover that overlaps nothing the layer draws changes nothing.** The layer keeps its single picture layer or visual; no tile exists (P13: zero cost when unused). A cover that overlaps only a mark changes only the mark pieces; the picture stays one layer or visual.
5. **A cover that overlaps the picture is cut out by splitting, not masking.** The picture is shown as one tile per region piece, each a rectangular clip around a picture element showing the same surface:
   - macOS: a tile is a sublayer of the preview layer with `masksToBounds` (a rectangular clip, which the window server does with a scissor, not an offscreen pass) holding a picture sublayer framed at the whole picture's rectangle, offset by the tile's origin, `contents` the frame's IOSurface. Tile and picture frames are in the preview layer's coordinates and follow its effective orientation (`contentsAreFlipped`, as the marks already do, ADR-0048). While tiles show, the preview layer's own `contents` is nil and each frame sets the `contents` of each tile (at most 72, typically 2 to 4) in the one transaction `show` already makes. No mask: `CALayer.mask` would cost an offscreen pass each frame while it is set.
   - Windows: a tile is a visual with a rectangle clip holding a visual with the picture's transform and the composition swapchain surface as content; `Present` updates every visual showing the surface. Microsoft documents that an `IDCompositionSurface` may be the content of several visuals, but not for a surface made by `CreateSurfaceFromHandle` for a composition swapchain, and a failure may draw black without any error. So Windows keeps step 1 for the picture (hide while a cover overlaps the picture) until a spike on Windows hardware shows the shared content, records it in this ADR's Review and turns the tiles on; rule 1 is not yet met on Windows until then, which the owner accepts by accepting this ADR. Marks are plain colour visuals and share no surface, so Windows clips them to the panel minus the covers at once; a cover over a mark only never hides the layer there either.
   - Switching between the single layer and tiles, and every change of tiles, happens in one transaction (macOS) or one `Commit` (Windows). A new tile gets the frame on screen at once; a tile no longer needed loses its content (`contents` nil, content `None`) so it holds no ring surface, and is kept for reuse.
   - The covers are read before Slint redraws, but the layer change and Slint's frame are separate commits, so for one display refresh a popup can open under the picture or a closed popup's place can stay empty. Accepted: it is one refresh, no worse than step 1.
6. **Separate windows are not covers.** Native menus, the Settings window, file dialogs, floating panels and projectors are OS windows, which the OS draws above the layer.
7. **`set_hidden` stays** for the cases where the picture really is not shown (the panel is not in the main window, the studio stopped) for a cover that is not finite (decision 3) and for the Windows interim of decision 5.
8. **Tests.**
   - Region function, over generated inputs: pieces are disjoint, inside the picture, never overlap a cover (covers snapped outward), cover every point of the picture outside the covers for up to five covers, and have snapped edges; no cover gives the picture itself; a non-finite cover hides. Mark clipping: a handle in the letterbox bar stays, a mark under a cover is cut.
   - macOS (`tests/share_layer_metal.rs`): tile and picture frames after `set_cutouts` with a flipped and an unflipped superlayer; tiles hold the shown frame at once; the single layer comes back and tiles lose their contents when the covers go.
   - Windows: a hardware spike first (decision 5), then a layer test like the macOS one.
   - Manual on the reference Mac: the colour picker, the panel menu over the preview and the add-source menu; the picture stays, the popup is fully drawn, no seam. CPU of the UI with a popup open over the picture against without, in one sitting, and whether the picture keeps direct-to-display or overlay promotion with tiles (Quartz Debug or Instruments), since tiles may lose it while a popup overlaps.

## Consequences

- Opening a picker, menu or toast no longer blanks the preview; covered parts are cut out exactly. With nothing over the picture the layer is unchanged and costs what it did.
- `rbs_gpu::share::PreviewLayer` gains `set_cutouts(&[LayerRect])` on both platforms, in the same units as `set_rect`. UI-side code, not the engine's hot path (`hot-paths.md` already excludes `layer.rs`).
- While a popup overlaps the picture on macOS, each frame sets a few `contents` instead of one; measured on the reference Mac with the implementation. On Windows a surface shown by several visuals is probably not promoted to a hardware overlay, so the desktop compositor composes it while a popup overlaps the picture; measured with the hardware spike.
- Windows does not meet rule 1 until the spike (decision 5): there the picture still hides while a cover overlaps it.
- The implementation updates `docs/specs/ui-design-system.md` (Program and Preview) and ADR-0048's statement that marks hide with the layer; this ADR adds the U21 bullet.
- Linux has no preview layer yet (deferred); when it gets one, it follows the same rule.

## Alternatives considered

- **Hide the whole layer, but only when a cover intersects the picture.** Fixes the reported case, but a menu that overlaps the picture by a few pixels still blanks it; the owner asked for never. It is step 1 and stays the Windows behaviour until the hardware spike (decision 5).
- **A `CALayer.mask` (or an even-odd shape layer) on macOS.** Simplest on macOS and also clips the marks, but the window server renders a masked layer offscreen every frame while the mask is set, and Windows has no equivalent (DirectComposition clips are rectangles), so the platforms would differ.
- **Each popup reports its rectangle under an id.** Works without Slint internals, but every popup, now and in the redesign, must remember to do it, and the position must be recomputed as Slint clamps it. Reading Slint's own list is exact and needs no per-popup code.
- **Draw popups as separate OS windows** (the other candidate of step 2). The OS stacks them above the layer, so the picture needs no change and nothing costs per frame. But Slint 1.18's winit backend cannot (`PopupWindowLocation::TopLevel` exists in `i-slint-core`, the winit backend never creates one), so every popup would become our own borderless Slint `Window` that we position over the main window, keep attached when it moves (macOS child window, Windows owned popup), close on an outside click, Escape or focus loss, and expose to screen readers as a menu, on each platform. Each open creates a native window and a GPU surface (several MiB and a visible delay on first open); popups of Slint's own widgets (`Select`, `ComboBox`, context menus) would still be in-window. Cost: weeks of platform code and a permanent maintenance surface against days for the split, which costs a few `contents` changes per frame on macOS while a popup overlaps the picture, and nothing otherwise. Revisit when Slint draws popups as separate windows; then decision 6 covers them.
- **The layer below a transparent toolkit window.** Correct stacking, but a permanent blending cost for the whole window (ADR-0034 alternatives).
- **Draw the picture with Slint.** Rejected by the UI CPU budget (ADR-0034, ADR-0036: 12-27 %).
- **Place popups so that they never overlap the picture.** Not always possible (small windows, large text) and makes popups jump.

## Review

- 2026-11-06, independent AI session (fresh context: proposal, cited ADRs and specs, layer and shell code, Slint sources), round 1: changes requested. Splitting confirmed as the right choice and cheaper than OS popup windows; `masksToBounds` avoids the mask's offscreen pass; P13 and P4 kept. Findings and resolution:
  1. Blocking: clipping marks to the picture region would cut handles in the letterbox bars. Resolved: marks are clipped to the panel minus the covers; a cover over a mark only changes mark pieces (decisions 3 and 4, test).
  2. Blocking: a runtime fallback on Windows has no trigger, since sharing may silently draw black, and the sharing is undocumented for composition swapchain surfaces. Resolved: Windows keeps step 1 until a hardware spike proves it; recorded as a known gap of rule 1 (decision 5, Consequences).
  3. Major: shell, Windows (`fit` + round) and macOS (gravity) fit the picture differently. Resolved: one snapped `fit` rectangle for the region and both layers (decision 3).
  4. Major: seams at fractional edges. Resolved: all edges snapped; a property of the region test.
  5. Major: macOS orientation unstated. Resolved: frames follow `contentsAreFlipped`; tested both ways.
  6. Major: the piece cap and its test were vague. Resolved: band algorithm named, exact up to four covers, cap 48 tiles and 128 mark pieces.
  7-11. Minor: tiles keep surfaces after use; Windows cost claim; spec and U21 not listed; one-refresh lag; future scrims. Resolved in decisions 2 and 5 and Consequences.
- 2026-11-06, independent AI session (fresh context), round 2: all round-1 findings resolved, band bound checked (2k + 1 bands of k + 1 intervals); changes requested on wording: the shell and the layer fitting different sizes (now: the layer computes the region from covers, everything fits the surface size), cover snapping direction (outward; shadows inside geometry), more than four covers (now exact up to five, cap 72, beyond that a stated limit), non-finite covers (hide), Windows marks in the interim (clipped at once), fixed-capacity scratch and overlay promotion in the measurement. All applied in decisions 3, 5 and 8.
- 2026-11-06, independent AI session (fresh context), round 3: approve. Minor wording applied: exceptions to rule 1 named in decision 1, the non-finite cover added to decision 7, where macOS gets its snapping scale, and the error bar named as `Shell.top-toast` in decision 2.
- Status stays Proposed: decision 5 leaves rule 1 unmet on Windows until a hardware check, which needs the owner's approval.
