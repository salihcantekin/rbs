## Context

RBS starts and stops recordings and streams but cannot pause them. OBS has Pause Recording, and users expect it. OBS has no way to pause a stream; users switch the program to a "Be right back" scene, which also changes the recording and takes the program away from the operator.

What the engine has today:

- Outputs are started and stopped one at a time (`Studio::start_output`, `stop_output`). A video encoder or audio encoder is shared between outputs of one canvas when their settings are equal (ADR-0047). The default recording (12 Mbit/s) and stream (6 Mbit/s) do not share.
- The video export already loads one atomic (`ExportEnd::at`) and compares each canvas time with it per tick, to end at a chosen time (ADR-0045). `u64::MAX` means "run".
- `EncoderChange::RequestKeyframe` exists in the codec port, gated by the capability `codec.reconfigure: keyframe`. NVENC, AMF and QSV (`rbs-codec-hw`), VideoToolbox and Media Foundation declare it. The video encoder node sends it only on its own carry-over overflow; nothing outside the node can ask for one (RTMP's `KeyframeRequested` is only counted, and ADR-0090's bitrate change needs the same path).
- Separate files (ADR-0086, ADR-0119) have their own exports and encoders; the replay buffer (ADR-0088) has its own export and encoder.
- The recording manifest and markers (ADR-0089) map master time from `t0` to positions in the files; the stop rewrite (stage 2, in progress) records each file's first sample and length from the output node.
- Rule 10 (P13): no per-iteration check of whether a feature is on in a hot loop; P15: hot-path changes need numbers and the maintainer's sign-off.

Stopping the data of a live stream does not work as a pause: services treat a silent connection as a lost or ended broadcast.

## Decision

### Recording

1. **Pause and resume.** A running recording can be paused and resumed any number of times. Paused, nothing reaches its files; resumed, it continues the same files with no gap in their timeline. Pause acts on every file of the recording at one instant: the main file, every separate video, audio and picture file (ADR-0086, ADR-0117). Stop while paused finalises at once; there is no tail to keep. The recording's encoder sessions stay open while paused (resume must be immediate and must not fail on a hardware session limit); the estimate (ADR-0087) still counts them.
2. **The window.** A pause is one immutable *window record* `{pause_at, resume_at, paused_total}` (`resume_at` is `u64::MAX` while paused), published by the control thread as a whole (a seqlock over two fixed slots preallocated with the output, written with Release and read with Acquire; no allocation or free on a worker thread, and nodes never read the three values separately). `pause_at` and `resume_at` are render tick times at least **two render periods after** the request, so a render, encoder or output worker already inside a tick cannot have taken the instant. Both lie on the frame grid: `paused_total` is a whole number of frames. A resume requested before `pause_at` is reached cancels the pause: no window, no keyframe, no manifest row. Requests in quick succession are applied in order; one still pending is replaced by the next. A window opens only on a running output that is not ending.
3. **Pause and end compose.** The export keeps one run-path atomic, `next`: the earliest of the end time (ADR-0045) and the next window instant, `u64::MAX` when neither is set. Both `end_at` and a window lower it with a compare-and-swap loop, never a blind store; only the end path sets `ended`, `take_oldest` and `ExportEnd::requested`, so a paused export is never read as ended by `output_tail_done` or by encoder sharing. Ticks at or past `next` take the slow path, which reads the end and the window record. Only the export raises `next` again once an instant has passed: by compare-and-swap from the value it observed to the earliest of the end time and the next window instant (`u64::MAX` for none); a failed swap means `end_at` lowered it meanwhile, and the lower value stays.
4. **Owned chains are cut before the encoder.**
    - *Video.* An export whose chain is the recording's alone (the main export, and each separate video file's export with its `every_tick` stamping) takes no canvas with a time in `[pause_at, resume_at)` and stamps later frames `t - paused_total`. While paused it does no readback and its encoder gets nothing.
    - *Audio.* The `AudioEncoder` contract counts output pts from the first block's timestamp by samples, so dropping samples closes the gap by itself: the gate drops the samples in the window (a block straddling an instant is split by sample count, the fractional remainder carried as an integer so audio never drifts more than one sample from video) and does not touch later timestamps. It fades 5 ms out before the cut and 5 ms in after it in a scratch buffer reserved in `prepare` (two blocks per join use it). The PCM adapter, which today re-reads a block's timestamp when its buffer empties (`rbs-codec-pcm`), is brought in line with the contract in phase 1, with a conformance test.
    - *Pictures (ADR-0117).* An image track writes nothing during a pause. At resume, if the source's picture differs from the last one written, it is taken again and written at the join.
    - *Keyframe at the join.* When the encoder's adapter declares `codec.reconfigure: keyframe`, the first frame after the join is requested as a keyframe (decision 7), so each segment starts cleanly for editors.
5. **Shared chains are cut at the recording's output node.** When an encoder of the recording also feeds another output (a stream with the same settings), the stream keeps it, so the recording's output node cuts the packets:
    - The pause is allowed only when that video encoder's adapter declares `codec.reconfigure: keyframe` (every built-in hardware encoder, VideoToolbox and Media Foundation do). Otherwise `pause_output` refuses with the reason (`KeyframeUnsupported`), so files never drift apart by a natural keyframe's delay.
    - Video: in decode order, packets from the first one whose pts is at or after `pause_at` are dropped (with B-frames, frames shown before `pause_at` that follow it in decode order are lost: at most the B-frame count). At resume the encoder is asked for a keyframe at the first frame with pts at or after `resume_at` (decision 7); packets pass again from that keyframe, pts and dts shifted by `paused_total`. The requested keyframe must start a closed GOP; pictures that follow it in decode order but would show before the join after the shift are dropped. Each passed packet's dts is clamped per packet to `max(dts + shift, last_dts + 1)` while that stays at or below its pts, and the clamp stays in force until the shifted dts catches up, so the muxer never sees `NonMonotonic` at a join.
    - Audio: packets with pts at or after `pause_at` are dropped; packets pass again from the first one with pts at or after `resume_at`, shifted by `paused_total`. The last packet kept before the cut gets its duration set to end at `pause_at`, or is dropped when that would leave it empty, so packets never overlap at the join. The join may be off by less than one audio packet (about 21 ms for AAC), always on the same side, which the manifest records.
6. **The host decides per chain, and a chain once paused is never shared.** The studio host picks decision 4 or 5 for each chain at every pause, from the sharing it tracks. A chain with an open window or with `paused_total > 0` carries shifted timestamps, so it is never shared with an output started later (the sharing filter of `start_output` checks it, as it checks an ended export, ADR-0045). Such an output opens its own encoder; the estimate (ADR-0087) counts that session, and a refusal for `codec.sessions.max` says the paused recording is why.
7. **Keyframe request at a time.** The video encoder node gets a request "keyframe at the first frame with pts at or after `t`" (an atomic time, `u64::MAX` for none, read once per tick and applied before `submit`). The studio passes the adapter's `codec.reconfigure` capability to the node when it builds it; without it the request is never stored. The node's existing request on carry-over overflow is unchanged. The same path can later serve RTMP `KeyframeRequested` and ADR-0090's bitrate changes, each under its own decision.
8. **Cost when unused: types first, then a measured exception.** The gates are type parameters chosen when a node is built: the export, the audio encoder node and the output node get `PauseGate` for a recording (and its separate files), `HoldGate` for a stream (decision 12), `NoGate` for the replay buffer and the virtual camera, which compiles to nothing. What a recording that is never paused still pays, and what this ADR asks the owner to accept as an exception to rule 10, measured with `measure-performance` against main before merge: the export, nothing new on the run path (the `next` compare replaces the end compare) plus one subtraction of a node-local shift; the audio encoder node, one relaxed load per 10 ms block; the output node, one relaxed load per tick; the video encoder node, one relaxed load per tick (decision 7). These are hot paths (P15): the `hot-path-review` checklist and the owner's sign-off are required.
9. **Vertical and several outputs.** Pause acts on the recording the caller names: the main one, the vertical one, or `all`. Pause in the frame's group acts on the recordings that Record started together (ADR-0111 flags).
10. **Manifest and editor project.** Every existing time in the manifest is in *file time*, with the pauses removed: `duration_ns`, `start_offset_ns`, image `at_ns`, marker `t_ns` and `pressed_ns`. So a format 1 reader that knows nothing of pauses still places every file, picture and marker right. Added keys: `paused_ns` and one `[[pauses]]` table per pause with `at_file_ns` (the join), `from_ns` and `to_ns` (master time from `t0`) and, for a shared cut, `join_error_ns`. A marker pressed while paused is placed on that pause's join with `while_paused = true`. A marker's lead (ADR-0089) is applied in file time and never reaches back past the join of the pause just before its press. The editor project writes one marker per pause on its join ("Paused 2:10"), switched by the recording setting "Pauses become markers in the editor project" (on by default). ADR-0089's span tracking reads the timestamps the output node writes, after the gate.
11. **Other features.** The replay buffer keeps running during a pause. Recording protection (ADR-0090) does not watch disk speed or bitrate while paused and computes "time left" from the recorded rate. "Record when the stream starts" is unchanged.

### Stream

12. **No pause of the data; a hold instead.** A live stream is never stopped by a pause. *Hold stream* keeps the connection and sends a hold picture with silence, while the program, the preview and the scenes stay free to edit; timestamps keep running. Resume stream returns to the program at the next frame. Streams are built with `HoldGate`: in the export and the audio encoder node it costs what `PauseGate` costs (decision 8).
13. **Hold picture, phase 2a.** `freeze` (default): the export stops reading back, keeps the last converted frame's staging buffer pinned (taken out of the ring, no copy) and hands it to the encoder on each tick re-stamped with the tick's time and a new sequence, so the encoder node submits it (it skips a repeated timestamp and sequence). `black`: one black frame converted once into a pinned buffer, the same way. The ring works one buffer short while holding. The audio encoder node submits silence from its scratch buffer in place of the mix. Phase 2b adds `scene`: a scene of the collection chosen in Settings, Stream, drawn by an extra compositor added to `RENDER` and linked to the stream's export only while holding, with that scene's sound when chosen; its sources open only then (P13).
14. **Hold with a shared encoder.** When the stream shares an encoder with a recording, holding the stream first pauses that recording (decision 5), so the hold picture never reaches the file. The host records that the hold paused it; Resume stream resumes the recording only then, never a recording the user had paused themselves. Keeping the recording of the program while a shared stream holds is not offered.
15. **Break.** One command holds every live stream and pauses every recording. If every output it covers is already in the break, it resumes them all; otherwise it puts the rest into the break.

### Control and UI

16. **Control API 1.57** (additive, a minor version; number final at merge). `record.pause {canvas?}`, `record.resume {canvas?}`, `record.pause_toggle {canvas?}` (`canvas` as in 1.29, `all` as in 1.42); `stream.hold {canvas?, picture?}` (`freeze`, `black`; `scene` once 2b exists), `stream.resume {canvas?}`, `stream.hold_toggle {canvas?}`; `output.break_toggle`. All `control` scope, live class; the stream methods change what is on air and take the on-air permission check. `conflict` when nothing runs to pause or hold, it is already in the asked state, or the pause is refused (`KeyframeUnsupported`, with its reason); `invalid_params` for an unknown `picture`. `record.state` and `record.canvas_state` add `paused`, `paused_ms`, `recorded_ms`; `state` stays `recording` while paused, so an older client never thinks the recording stopped. `stream.state` and `stream.canvas_state` add `held` and `held_ms`. Every method is a registry command (ADR-0050, ADR-0060), so shortcuts (global allowed), the RBS menu, Stream Deck and MCP get it. The obs-websocket compatibility layer, when built, maps `PauseRecord`, `ResumeRecord`, `ToggleRecordPause` and the paused output state.
17. **UI (visual approval before merge, rule 16).** A round Pause button beside Stop recording, only while a recording runs; paused, it becomes Resume and the indicator reads PAUSED with the stopped recorded time in the warning colour, never the live red. A round Hold button beside End stream while live; holding, the indicator reads ON HOLD. The status bar shows the paused or held time. Live controls get the same commands; vertical chips get Pause in their menu. Settings, Stream: what the hold sends (picture, sound). Settings, Recording: the pause-marker switch. A refused pause shows its reason in the toast. Glossary: Pause recording, Resume recording, Hold stream, Resume stream, Break.

## Consequences

- Pausing matches OBS and goes past it: a recording that shares its encoder with the stream can pause, owned encoders do no work while paused, separate files pause together, and every join shows in the editor project.
- Streams can take a break without ending the broadcast or touching the program.
- New public contract: `rbs-record` (`pause_output`, `resume_output`, hold calls, `Progress` paused fields), io-node behaviour (export window, audio encoder gate, output gate, encoder keyframe request), manifest keys, control API 1.57. Each needs this ADR's independent review.
- Hot-path changes in the export, video and audio encoder nodes and the output node: numbers with `measure-performance` and the `hot-path-review` checklist, then the owner's sign-off.
- Tests, all on `TestClock`, no sleeps: A/V offset after three pauses within one sample and one frame; instants on and inside a block; a control-thread store during a running `process` at the tick boundary (the window is never torn and the instant is never taken); a resume before `pause_at` cancels; quick toggles; dts monotonic across a B-frame join on a shared chain; a shared encoder without the capability refuses the pause; a chain once paused is not shared with a later output; the PCM adapter across a pause; stop while paused and stop during a resume (end and window compose); separate video, audio and picture files sharing the join; markers while paused and a lead across a join; manifest times in file time read correctly by the current `rbs-project`; FCPXML pause markers; hold keeps timestamps, connection and the pinned buffer; holding a shared stream pauses and resuming resumes the recording.
- The video encoder node's external keyframe request is a new hot-path input; RTMP and ADR-0090 reuse it only after their own decisions.
- Follow-ups not decided here: "Resume into a new file" as an option; a time limit or auto-resume for hold; replay buffer pause.

## Alternatives considered

- **Cut always at the output node.** One place for every case, but owned encoders would keep encoding paused content, and resume would always need a keyframe. Rejected: wasted GPU and encoder work (rule 17).
- **Cut always before the encoder.** Cheapest, but impossible while the encoder also feeds a stream. OBS takes this path and refuses to pause in that case. Rejected.
- **Insert a gate node into the graph at pause time.** No new check on the run path, but every pause would relink the graph mid-recording, with a glitch risk at the moment the user wants a clean cut. Rejected.
- **A file per segment.** Simple, but the user asked for OBS's behaviour (one file). Kept as a later option.
- **Stop sending on a stream pause.** Services end or degrade the broadcast. Rejected.
- **Switch the program to a "BRB" scene.** Possible today by hand; it changes the recording and the preview and takes the program away. Hold is the better default; the hold scene (2b) gives the same look without those costs.

## Review

Independent review per ADR-0014.

- **Round 1** (independent AI session, 2026-11-15): changes requested, 10 major and 2 minor. Resolved in the revision: the pause window is its own record and composes with the end through `next` (1); the window is one record, two periods ahead, with cancel and ordering rules (2); a chain once paused is never shared (3); a shared cut needs the keyframe capability, dts and audio rules stated (4); the keyframe request carries a time and the adapter's capability (5); the cost is stated as an exception to rule 10 for the owner (6); streams get `HoldGate` and the held frame is re-stamped from a pinned buffer (7); the audio cut follows the sample-count contract, with the PCM fix first (8); separate files and picture tracks covered (9); manifest times in file time (10); API targets, semver and Break semantics (11); tests (12).
- **Round 2** (same reviewer, 2026-11-15): two small fixes left, approve after them. Resolved: per-packet dts clamp and closed GOP at a shared join (A); only the export raises `next`, by compare-and-swap (B); Resume stream resumes only a recording its hold paused (C); the last audio packet before a shared cut is trimmed (D); seqlock over fixed slots; the extra encoder session a later output needs. Open: the owner's written acceptance of the rule-10 exception in decision 8 (every recording and every stream pays one relaxed load per audio block, per output tick and per video encoder tick; measured before merge).
- **Owner:** Salih accepted the ADR and the rule-10 exception of decision 8, 2026-11-15 ("tamamdır kabul ediyorum. Accepted et"). The exception's numbers are still measured before the implementation merges (P15).
- **Implementation note** (2026-11-15, recorded while building phase 1; the decision is unchanged, one statement of decision 4 was wrong): the audio bullet of decision 4 says the gate "does not touch later timestamps" because the `AudioEncoder` trait documents pts counted from the first block. The output ports spec, invariant 8, is the binding contract and says otherwise: every adapter (AudioToolbox, Media Foundation, FFmpeg AAC, FLAC, PCM) treats a gap of one codec frame or more as a discontinuity and restarts its timeline, padding with silence. So the audio gate stamps the blocks after a cut earlier by the dropped samples' duration, and the encoder sees one continuous timeline, as the export does for video. The PCM change in decision 4 and step 18 is then not needed: with continuous timestamps PCM is correct. The audio-graph and recording specs say this.
