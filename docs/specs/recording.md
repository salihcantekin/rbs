# Spec: Recording a canvas

- Status: Draft
- Owner crate(s): `rbs-record`, `rbs-cli`
- Related ADRs: [0004](../adr/0004-single-master-clock.md), [0013](../adr/0013-zero-cost-when-unused.md), [0026](../adr/0026-partitions-and-queues.md), [0027](../adr/0027-engine-loop.md), [0037](../adr/0037-audio-graph.md), [0038](../adr/0038-io-graph-nodes.md), [0039](../adr/0039-scene-source-types.md), [0040](../adr/0040-recording-composition.md), [0041](../adr/0041-studio-recording-on-a-running-engine.md), [0047](../adr/0047-several-outputs-per-canvas.md), [0102](../adr/0102-output-size-distinct-from-canvas.md)
- Builds on: [scene sources](scene-sources.md), [scene model](scene-model.md) (binder), [I/O nodes](io-nodes.md), [audio graph](audio-graph.md), [engine](engine.md), [output ports](output-ports.md)

## Purpose

Records the program scene of one canvas of a scene collection to a file, or streams it live: the sources the scene shows and plays, composited, encoded (video and audio) and muxed (or handed as packets to a transport that frames them, such as RTMP), with every stream on the master clock. `rbs-record` assembles and runs it on the engine; `rbs-cli record` and `rbs-cli stream` are the headless commands that register the platform's adapters and build a fixed scene. A `Studio` keeps the canvas running for a UI (preview, meters, scene edits) and starts and stops outputs (recordings and live streams, up to two at once, ADR-0047) on the running engine (ADR-0041); `Recording` is a studio without a preview plus one recording.

## Scope

In scope: what `Recording::start` builds and how it chooses adapters, the partitions, stream order and timestamps, start and run-time failures, progress counters, the `rbs-cli` commands; the `Studio`: preview, meters, scene edits and outputs started and stopped on a running engine, several at once (recording and streaming), with encoders shared when their settings match.

A live stream is the same graph with a network transport: the output's URL, transport input, secret and reconnect policy differ, nothing else.

Out of scope: more than `MAX_OUTPUTS` outputs, recording and streaming at once from `rbs-cli`, scene documents on disk, the control API, end-of-stream flushing of encoders (engine spec, open question).

### The graph

```
sources --video--> compositor --canvas--> video export ==video==> video encoder ==packets==> output s0
 (render)           (render)              (render)                (video encoder)            (output)
audio inputs --audio--> mixer ==audio==> audio encoder ==packets==> output s1
 (audio)                (audio)          (audio encoder)
```

| Partition | Runtime | Pacing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `RENDER` | `GpuRuntime` on the given device | the canvas frame rate from time 0 |
| `partitions.audio` | `()` | `Pacing::stream(audio_graph.cadence(now), 4)` |
| `partitions.video_encoder` | `()` | the canvas frame rate, a quarter frame after render |
| `partitions.audio_encoder` | `()` | doorbell (one tick per queued block), 100 ms heartbeat |
| `partitions.output` | `()` | `Pacing::output()` |

Without audio (`audio: None`) the two audio partitions are not added: three worker threads. Without video (`video: None`) no export and no video encoder are added, so that partition's worker does not start. Both `None` is `EmptyOutput` before anything is chosen.

### `Recording::start`

1. The canvas must exist (`UnknownCanvas`) and have a program scene (`NoProgram`). When `video` is set, the export is NV12 (or `video.format`) at the output size: `OutputSettings::size`, or the canvas size when `size` is `None` (ADR-0102). An explicit size that is odd or outside 2..=7680 fails with `RecordError::OutputSize { width, height }` before any adapter is chosen. Absent size skips that check, so a canvas side above 7680 (a vertical canvas up to 8192) still encodes. The start does not re-fit the aspect: ADR-0102 decision 4 is the size a later settings UI stores. A size whose aspect differs from the canvas is letterboxed by the scaled download (`fit_rect`: aspect kept, centred, black outside). Equal to the canvas uses the 1:1 download (`YuvDownload::with_tone`); a different size scales once from that canvas (`scaled_tone`).
2. Adapters are chosen before anything opens, each from its registry's `select`, best first (user choice, preference order, hardware first, registration order):
   - video encoder (when `video` is set): `codec.video.encode` includes the codec, `input.memory` includes `system`, `input.pixel_format` includes the export format; the first candidate whose `VideoEncoderNode::open` succeeds with every `VideoSettings` field in its `VideoEncoderConfig` (rate control, keyframe interval, B-frames, profile, `tuning.preset`), so a candidate that refuses a setting (a profile it lacks, `cbr_padded` or constant quality on an encoder without it) is skipped with its reason in `tried`;
   - audio encoder (when `audio` is set): `codec.audio.encode` includes the codec; the first whose session is created for `audio_graph.format` and the bitrate. When the codec is PCM or FLAC and `bit_depth` is set, that width is the `bit_depth` adapter option (`"16"`, `"24"` or, for PCM only, `"32"`). Absent is 16-bit. Other codecs are not given the option;
   - muxer (container input only; none for packet input, `StartReport::muxer` is `None`): `mux.container` includes the container; the best;
   - transport: `transport.schemes` includes the URL's scheme (lower case) and `transport.input` includes the input's capability value (`packets`, or `container.transport_input()`); the best. It is created with the URL, input, secret, reconnect policy and options of the settings and the default queue limits (output ports spec).
   No candidate: `NoAdapter { port, tried }`, with each rejected or failed adapter and why. A URL without a scheme is `InvalidUrl`.
3. The collection is bound with `SceneBinder::new(sources.node_types())`, `.with_audio(partitions.audio)` only when audio is recorded. A `BindProblem` fails the start (`Problems`) only when it leaves out a source the program scene shows or, with audio, plays: an unknown source type, or a source with no video output when video is what it contributes (an audio-only source without audio is simply not recorded). Problems anywhere else in the collection go to `StartReport::problems` and the recording goes on.
4. Only the sources the output's plan schedules are instantiated (`SourceTypes::instantiate` with the engine's clock and `audio_graph`), in plan order: sources of other scenes or canvases, and audio sources when audio is off, are never opened. An instantiation error fails the start (`Source`). Video sources go to `RENDER`, audio-only sources to `partitions.audio` (the binder chose).
5. When `video` is set, the export and the video encoder are added Active and linked to the program compositor of the canvas, and the output's first stream is video. The audio encoder, when `audio` is set, links to the canvas's mixer and to the output's next stream (`s0` when there is no video, `s1` when there is). The compositor's layers come from `node_layers` over the final plan; mixer strips from `report.mixers[canvas].inputs`, each with its source's volume and mute, with a level meter, and a master meter. With no video the compositor has no sink, so picture sources stay closed (P13).
6. The partitions are added and every instance adopted, then the graph is applied once. If the compositor, mixer, export, an encoder or the output did not join, or the engine reported errors or links that could not cross partitions, the engine is shut down and the start fails with `Start(Vec<StartFailure { node, reason }>)`, which also lists the shutdown's errors (node `"shutdown"`). The output may have opened the destination by then; removing a file it created is the composition root's job. A source that did not join (a camera another program holds) is recorded in `StartReport::sources[i].failed` and the recording goes on without it.

### Running and stopping

- `service` runs `Engine::service` and reads its events, matched to sources by `SourceState::node`: a `Failed` event of a source sets that source's `failed`, `Restarted` or `Recovered` clears it; a permanent failure (`retry_at: None`) of an essential node sets `failure`. `failure()` also returns the output's first permanent failure cause (`OutputNodeStats::failure`). The composition root stops when `failure()` is set.
- `progress` has the output's connection state: `connects` (times the transport reached its destination, reconnections included; 0: nothing was ever delivered), `reconnect_attempts`, `connected` (now) and `transport_drops` (items a network transport dropped by its drop policy), from `OutputNodeStats`.
- `progress` counts video frames along the chain: `frames_exported` (canvas frames the export read back), `export_drops` (dropped at the export), `encoder_skipped` (exported frames a newer frame replaced in the one-frame queue into the video encoder's partition before the encoder took them, or that were still queued at stop: `QueueStats::video_drops`), `frames_encoded` and `encoder_drops` (busy, refused or lost in the carry-over). The queues into the encoders' partitions are found after the apply that schedules them and kept with the recording, so the final `Progress` still has their counters after the draining apply removed them (`encoder_skipped`, `audio_queue_drops`).
- `finish` shuts the engine down (sources first, the output last, which writes the container's trailer and finishes the transport) and returns the final `Progress`, `failure`, shutdown errors, `flush_lost` (packets the video encoder still held after its final-tick flush, ADR-0043; normally 0) and `finish_lost` (output items not delivered). Call `end` first and service until `tail_done` to keep the frames still read back (ADR-0045). `Progress::start_drops` counts audio packets dropped by the output's aligned start.

### Studio (ADR-0041)

1. `Studio::start` checks the canvas (`UnknownCanvas`, `NoProgram`), binds the collection (`with_audio(partitions.audio)` when `settings.audio`), adds `RENDER` and the video encoder and output partitions, and with audio the audio and audio encoder partitions (pacing as in the table above), and adopts a compositor (`MAX_LAYERS` layers) and, with audio, the canvas's mixer (`MAX_LAYERS` strips, a master meter). With `settings.preview` a `PreviewNode` (I/O nodes spec) is linked to the compositor's `canvas` on `RENDER`; with audio a `MeterSinkNode` (audio graph spec) to the mixer's `main` on the audio partition, sent to the engine only while the audio branch is awake (5c). These are the studio's nodes: if one does not start, the engine is shut down and the start fails with `Start`.
2. Sources: the sources the plan schedules are instantiated and adopted (capture, still, media file, browser page and text to `RENDER`, audio-only to the audio partition); a source that cannot be instantiated or does not start is reported in `sources()[i].failed` and the studio runs without it. Binder problems are reported in `problems()`, never fatal. Without a preview the compositor has no sink, so video sources are opened only while a recording runs; without audio no audio source is opened (P13). The compositor gets `node_layers` of the plan and the mixer one strip per input of `report.mixers[canvas]`, with the gain and mute of its source's `audio` (ADR-0059), each with a level meter (`meter(source)`), through their parameter channels.
2b. **Text sources (ADR-0129).** The first text instance makes the studio's `TextWorker` (the registered renderers, the engine's clock, `set_text_io(files, wall)` or the real file system and wall clock). Each text instance is adopted into `RENDER` and its picture sender handed to the worker (`attach`); `report.changed_sources` send the leniently read settings to the worker (`set_settings`) and the node stays; a text node that no longer runs is detached. After every reconcile and on `set_text_watched(sources)` (open settings windows) the on-air set is recomputed (`rbs_sources::text::text_on_air`: program, preview and globals scenes of each canvas, hold scenes, separate picture tracks while an output runs, watched sources; hidden items and nested scenes included); each text entering it is `refresh`ed and its request remembered. `text_ready()` is true once every entering text's node has uploaded that request. `text_prepare(canvas, scene)` comes before a program switch: the texts the scene places that are not on air yet (hidden, nested and global items included) join the on-air set and are `refresh`ed now; it returns true when the switch should be held back. The host then asks `text_due()` from its control loop: `Ready` once every entering text is uploaded, `Late` after `TEXT_WAIT_TICKS` (2) render ticks (counted in `text_late()`; the late texts are composed at their last picture until theirs is uploaded), `Wait` otherwise. The app holds a program switch this way (`held_switch`: a newer switch replaces it; the loop looks every 4 ms while one is held); without text sources `text_prepare` is false at once and nothing changes. In studio mode the preview is already on air, so a transition never waits. Hold start and take start do not wait yet: that comes with the live kinds (ADR-0129 phase (d)), the first texts whose picture changes while off air. `source_adjusted()` lists `SourceSettingsAdjusted { source, key }` for every text source, recomputed at each start and update: the keys `read_text_lenient` changed, and `video_as` when a text that is not still is marked `image` (the take runs it as `auto`: `picture_role` gives it a video track instead of refusing with `ImageOnMovingSource`). The document keeps them until the user edits the source. `iso_video_records(source)` is true while a running take writes a separate picture track of the source; the app then refuses a settings edit that changes a text's `width` or `height` ("Stop recording to change the box"), and an undo or redo that would change one is stepped back and refused with the same reason (ADR-0129 decision 5). Tests: `a_switch_to_a_scene_with_a_text_waits_for_its_new_picture`, `a_text_read_from_a_document_is_adjusted_and_reported`; `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/record.rs` `a_text_box_is_locked_while_a_take_records_its_picture_separately` (the app's refusal, for an edit and for an undo). `service` runs the worker's tick and collects its problems; `sources()` shows a text source's file or render problem as `failed` with its fault (file problems first) and a persistent texture failure of the node; `text_problem(source)` also gives notes (`FontMissing`). `text_fonts(sources)` gives the cached font list (`Ready`), starting the worker on first use (`NotReady` until the first list exists; `Unavailable` with no renderer). `shutdown` stops the worker first. Test: `crates/rbs-record/tests/text_source.rs`.
3. `update(collection, sources)`: the canvas must keep its size and rate (`NotBound`) and have a program scene (`NoProgram`), both checked before anything changes. The binder syncs; new sources get entries, retired ones are taken back from their partitions after the apply; a replaced source (settings changed) is applied break-before-make (one apply without the new node first). An instance whose take fails (its partition's swap still in flight) is kept and `service` retries it; a replacement is not instantiated while the instance it replaces is not taken back (`sources()[i].failed` says it waits, `waiting()` is true; an `update` with the same collection opens it once `waiting()` is false). Scheduled sources without an instance are instantiated (a source's first failure reason is kept), the layers are sent, and the graph is applied once. Strips: before the apply the mixer gets the strips of the old and the new inputs, after it only the new ones, so neither schedule feeds an input without a strip (`unknown_inputs` stays 0). Layers are sent before the apply: for up to one tick the old schedule may draw without a layer for a source that just left (skipped) and the new one without a layer for a source that just joined (not drawn yet); a producer is never drawn in the wrong place. Returns problems and the nodes that did not join.
2a. Filters (ADR-0062, [video-filters.md](video-filters.md)): every scheduled filter node gets a `FilterNode` in `RENDER`, created with its source's picture size when the studio has seen it (every filter of a chain gets it), its values from `filter_values_lenient` (adjustments in `filter_problems()`); `changed_filters` of an update send the new values through the node's channel without rebinding; retired filter instances are taken back like sources. `preview_filter(filter, values)` sends values to a running filter without changing the collection (a slider drag) and returns whether one got them; `restore_filter(filter)` sends the collection's values again (a cancelled drag); `filter_stats(filter)` gives a running instance's counters.
3a. `set_mix(collection)` (ADR-0059): for a change of a source's volume or mute alone. It reads each source's `audio` from the collection, rebuilds the mixer parameters from the strips the studio already holds and sends them through the mixer's parameter channel; nothing is bound, instantiated or applied. Returns false when the studio has no mixer. The mixer ramps a gain change over 10 ms, so a fader drag does not click.
4. `start_output(adapters, out)` (ADR-0047): `TooManyOutputs` while `MAX_OUTPUTS` run or the output's canvas already feeds `MAX_OUTPUTS_PER_CANVAS`, before anything is chosen; `NoEncoderSlot` when the output needs a video chain of its own and its canvas runs `MAX_CHAINS_PER_CANVAS` video chains, or every one of the `MAX_CHAINS` chains of a kind it needs is taken, before that encoder session opens (an output that shares a running chain is not counted); `EmptyOutput` when neither video nor audio is set; `NotBound("mixer")` for audio on a studio without audio. When `video` is set, the output size is resolved as in `Recording::start` step 1 (`OutputSize` before any adapter; an output already started keeps the size it started with, and this call does not restart the studio or move scene items). Adapters are chosen as in `Recording::start` step 2 before anything is added, except that a running encoder is reused (see "Several outputs"). The export is created at the resolved size only when `video` is set. Then the export (`RENDER`) and video encoder (with `out.video`), the audio encoder (with `out.audio`), each unless shared, and the output are added Active and linked (output streams: video, then audio; audio alone is `s0`), sources only now scheduled are opened, layers are sent and the graph is applied once. The new nodes are added to a copy of the graph and adopted all or nothing, so a failure there leaves the studio as it was. If one of the new nodes did not join, or the engine reported errors or links that could not cross partitions (in any partition), the new nodes are removed again (plain apply) and taken back, and the call fails with `Start` (removal errors listed with node `"rollback"`); the studio and the other output run on. The output may have created the destination.
5. `stop_output(id)`: the output's node, and every encoder chain no other running output uses, are removed and the graph applied with `Engine::apply_draining`: the partitions this empties run their final ticks in pipeline order (an unshared encoder its final tick and flush, the output drains its queue and writes the trailer in `release`) and stop; a shared encoder keeps running for the other output untouched (its frames in flight at that moment do not reach the stopped output; `flush_lost` is 0). Render and audio keep running when the preview, the meter sink (while awake, 5c) or the other output keeps them scheduled. Instances are taken back. Returns a `FinishReport` (its `shutdown_errors` are the apply's errors), or `None` when `id` does not run.
4a. `start_outputs(adapters, outs)` (ADR-0127 decision 3): each entry is checked and prepared as by `start_output`, in order (so a later entry shares a chain an earlier one of the same call opens), added to the graph, and then everything is applied once: outputs that share an encoder start on its same first keyframe. One result per entry, in order. An entry refused before the apply fails alone. After the apply, an output whose own nodes, or a chain it takes packets from, did not join is removed (plain apply, chains no remaining output uses go with it) and fails with `Start` listing those nodes' roles and reasons (and `"rollback"` errors); every other new output runs on. Only an engine or link failure of the apply removes every new output and fails each with the same `Start`. Running outputs are never touched.
5a. `stop_outputs(ids)`: `stop_output` for several outputs in one draining apply (ADR-0127 decision 7: End stream stops every destination at once, and a shared encoder no output keeps is flushed once). Ids that do not run are skipped; each report carries the apply's errors.
5b. `abort_output(id)` (ADR-0127 decision 5): for an output that failed or stalls. Sets the output node's abandon switch (I/O nodes spec, output node) and removes it, with the chains only it used, by a plain apply: its held and backlogged items are not delivered (counted in `finish_lost`), its muxer gets no `finish` and its transport is dropped without `Transport::finish` (its `Drop` is bounded, output ports spec), so the control thread is never held by a drain. A shared encoder is untouched and not flushed (`flush_lost` 0). `None` when `id` does not run.
5a. **Keeping the tail** (ADR-0045, per output). `end_output(id)` ends the output's video at the master clock's now. When its video chain is its alone, the export ends (`ExportEnd::end_at`): it takes no later canvas and passes on every frame it still reads back, and `output_tail_done(id)` is true once the export reports `ended` and the video encoder accepted the export's last frame (`last_frame` of both). A shared export keeps running for the other output, and `output_tail_done(id)` is true once the encoder accepted a frame at or after the end. Either way it is also true `END_TAIL_LIMIT` after the end on the master clock, and when `id` does not run; false while the output runs and was not ended. An output with no video has no picture to keep: `output_tail_done` is true once `end_output` was called. An export asked to end is not shared with an output started after that. The composition root keeps calling `service` until then and calls `stop_output`; an unshared video encoder's final tick takes the last frame from the queue, and an unshared audio encoder flushes on its last final tick (audio graph spec). Audio keeps flowing until the drain, so the file's audio may end a few frames after its last picture. `rbs-cli` (when the recording did not fail) and the desktop app's Stop and close do this for the recording; `stop_output` alone loses the frames still read back.
6. `service()` maps engine events as `Recording::service` does; a permanent failure of a studio node sets `failure()`; of an output node, that output's `output_failure(id)` only; of an encoder chain's node (export, video encoder, audio encoder), `output_failure` of every output using it. `output_failure` also returns the output's first permanent failure (`OutputNodeStats::failure`). The composition root stops an output when its `output_failure` is set.
5c. **Audio branch rest (ADR-0169).** `StudioSettings::audio_awake` (default `true`) and `set_audio_awake(bool) -> Vec<StartFailure>` decide whether the meter sink is in the graph the engine gets. The studio's own graph keeps the sink for its life, and everything the studio derives from a plan (which entries and filters are `scheduled`, which sources are instantiated with their meters, strips and input offsets, `sources()`, `source_health()`) follows that graph: `scheduled` means scheduled by the studio's model, not running. Every apply of the studio, its own graph or a trial graph (break before make, monitor, loudness, separate files, sync probes, the virtual camera, outputs), goes through one private `send_graph`, which removes the sink while resting and records the sources and pictures whose nodes failed to join (the first reason stays). Resting with no other reader of the mixer, the engine's plan leaves out the mixer, its inputs, their filters and the media and browser sound halves that only feed it; they are released and kept idle (an input's `release` stops its stream), and the audio partition has no worker. A reader that appears schedules them again from the kept instances; a device that does not start then reports its fault on its source. When the last output that read the mixer stops while resting, the audio partition empties in that `apply_draining` and drains in pipeline order. A repeat of the current state applies nothing. `set_meters_on` is separate and only puts the meters in the mixer's parameters or takes them out.
7. `shutdown()` stops every output in one draining apply (as `stop_output` does for one, so a shared encoder is drained and flushed for all of them), then shuts the engine down; the reports are in start order. Every call runs on the control thread.
8. Sync offsets: `settings.audio_offsets` holds a sync offset in ns per audio source (positive delays it, negative advances it; audio graph spec, "Offset", clamps it). An audio source's offset is sent on its input's offset channel before the node is adopted, so its first block already has it; this also holds for a source opened later or rebound after a settings change. `set_audio_offset(source, ns)` updates the setting (0 removes it) and sends it to a running input of that source (a change below 5 ms slews, a larger one resyncs once), returning whether one got it. `RecordSettings::audio_offsets` is passed to the studio by `studio()`. The desktop app fills `settings.audio_offsets` from the collection's `sync_offset_ms` (ADR-0062 decision 10) and calls `set_audio_offset` when one changes, so the collection is its one source of truth.

### Further canvases (ADR-0093, first step)

- **What runs.** Each `StudioSettings::extra_canvases` entry adds one compositor on `RENDER` at the canvas's own size and one picture (`canvas_preview`). Source nodes and their filter chains are built once by the binder and feed every compositor, so a device is opened once whatever the number of canvases. No entry: nothing of the others is scheduled (Installed, P13).
- **Checks at start.** `Studio::start` fails with `TooManyCanvases` (more than `MAX_CANVASES`, a canvas listed twice or equal to the main one), `UnknownCanvas`, `NoProgram`, or `CanvasRate` (the frame rate differs from the main canvas, ADR-0093 decision 2). `update` rejects a size or rate change of any running canvas.
- **Live.** `activate_canvas` adds a further canvas to a running studio (the checks of `start`, then an `update` that adopts its compositor and optional picture) and `deactivate_canvas` removes it again (`CanvasBusy` while an output takes it; its nodes leave the engine after the apply). `set_canvas_picture` gives a running further canvas its panel's picture (one slot; projector pictures use `add_picture`, see Pictures), or takes it away, also while its outputs run: the app adds it while the canvas's panel shows and removes it when the panel closes, so a closed panel adds no preview node and, without an output, no compositor pass (P13). Asking for what the canvas already has changes nothing; `UnknownCanvas` when it does not run. When the update fails after its checks, the change is kept and applies with the next update. The app does not ask again for a picture that did not start until its panel is hidden and shown, and hiding RBS's windows takes the picture away. Sources it shows open as in any update; open ones are shared.
- **Outputs.** `OutputSettings::canvas` (`None`: main) picks the canvas an output records or streams; an unknown one fails with `UnknownCanvas`. An encoder is shared only between outputs of the same canvas, with equal settings and equal resolved output size (ADR-0102). `MAX_OUTPUTS` is 14 in total and each canvas feeds at most `MAX_OUTPUTS_PER_CANVAS` (7) of them, on at most `MAX_CHAINS_PER_CANVAS` (4) video encoder chains (ADR-0093 decision 6, ADR-0127 decision 2: a recording and up to six stream destinations, which share the stream's encoder unless they have their own encoding): an eighth output of one canvas fails with `TooManyOutputs` before anything is chosen, a fifth own encoding with `NoEncoderSlot` before its session opens. The third and fourth output slots use `Partitions::more` (`VIDEO_ENCODER_3`..`OUTPUT_4`, partitions 11 to 16), output slots and chains 4 and up `Partitions::pool` (partitions 35 to 52); like the others they get a worker only while something is scheduled on them.
- **Stream destinations (ADR-0127 decision 1).** The output profile's `[stream-destinations]` section (`rbs-app` `stream_destinations`) holds `format_version = 1`, `next_id` and `[[destination]]` entries: `id` (`u32`, unique across the profile, never reused; `next_id` starts at 2), `canvas` (`main` or `vertical`), `name`, `service` (a preset id or `custom`; data only), `enabled` (default true), `server`, `key_saved`, `reconnect` (default true), `srt_latency_ms`, `encoding` (`mode = "shared"`, or `mode = "own"` with an `encoding` table and an optional `output_size`) and an optional `order` (ADR-0148 decision 5: the list sorts by it; an entry without one has its place in id order, the first slot first; a new destination gets the next order, so it is listed last). Ids 0 and 1 are the main and vertical canvas's first destinations: their server, key flag, reconnect and SRT latency stay in `stream` and `stream-vertical` (fields given here for them are ignored and not written), and their entry only names them, disables them or sets their encoding mode; an entry for 1 on the main canvas (or 0 on the vertical) is refused. A first destination exists while its stream section has a server (ADR-0148 decision 2); one without is not listed, not counted and never started. At most 6 destinations per canvas, an existing first one included, and at most 2 per canvas with their own encoding. Keys stay in the credential store: the first destinations under today's accounts (`stream_key::account` and its `/vertical`), every other one under `<account>/dest-<id>`; removing a destination deletes its key first and changes the list only once that worked; removing a first destination also empties its stream section's server, key flag, SRT latency and reconnect (back to on), and the next destination added to that canvas fills the slot, after its old key is deleted (ADR-0148 decisions 3 and 4). Streaming is available on a canvas while one of its existing destinations is on (`Destinations::available`). A section written by a newer RBS (`format_version` above 1), or one that does not load or breaks a rule above, reads as no extra destinations with the reason, and is not overwritten. **Go live (host, ADR-0127 decisions 3 to 9 and 13).** The UI hands the section to the host (`Command::SetStreamDestinations`; a section that does not load shows its reason as the stream settings' note). Go live of a canvas sends to its first destination while it exists (from the request the UI or the API built from `stream`, unless its entry disables it) and to every other enabled destination; without a first destination the request carries only the shared settings (`StreamRequest::from_settings`) and each destination gives its server; with no destination on, Go live and `stream.start` refuse with "Streaming is off" (ADR-0148 decision 6): each reads its own key, a destination with no server is kept as failed ("Not set up"), and with several destinations a missing or unreadable key fails that destination alone (with one, the answers stay as before: `NoKey`, or the read error). Destinations sharing the stream's encoding start in one `Studio::start_outputs` apply; each with its own encoding starts after on its own chain (`encoder`, `video_settings`, `audio_bps` and `output_size` of its `Encoding`). The stream goes live when one starts; each that did not is named in a notice. The host keeps one running destination as the stream's anchor (its output carries the hold and the stream's counters) and a state per destination (`connecting`, `live`, `reconnecting`, `failed`, `stopped`). Each round, a destination whose output failed is removed with `abort_output` and named in a notice; when the anchor fails the next running destination becomes the anchor; when none is left the stream ends with the last reason. The stream's link is connected while one destination is, its `connects` and `reconnect_attempts` the sums. End stream stops every destination in one `stop_outputs` apply. Hold and release act on the anchor's chain (every destination sharing it) and, in the same control-thread call, on each destination with its own chain; a shared hold switch for those chains (one atomic store) is a follow-up. Control API 1.74 (control-api spec, "Protocol 1.74") lists the destinations, starts or stops one (also while live: a destination joins the shared encoder at its next keyframe or opens its own chain; one stopped drains alone, and the anchor moves on) and reports each one's state in `stream.state`. The Settings UI follows.
- **With the main outputs (ADR-0111).** The output profile's `[outputs-vertical]` section holds `format_version = 1`, `record_with_main` and `stream_with_main` (both `true` when the section or a key is absent; `rbs-app` `vertical_outputs`). While a vertical canvas exists and a flag is true, Record or Go live starts the main output and then the vertical one of that kind, and stops both; a vertical stream with no server saved is skipped. A section written by a newer RBS, or one that does not load, reads as the defaults and is not overwritten until the user changes a flag. The flags stay out of `recording-vertical` and `stream-vertical`, so writing them never counts as saving vertical encoding settings.
- **Not yet.** Audio per canvas, Auto layout and the control API are the next steps of ADR-0093 decision 11; a follower canvas (ADR-0093 decision 3) shows the followed canvas's program scene: `Studio::start` and `update` set each follower's program scene to `SceneCollection::followed_programs` on a copy of the collection before binding, so the binder knows nothing of following. `Fallback::Black`, the transition rule (the follower cuts when the main canvas's timed transition ends), the "main canvas wins" frame skip of ADR-0093 decision 10 are not in yet; the set of running canvases is a `StudioSettings` field only (not yet saved with the workspace). The render tick is a hot path: measurements and the maintainer's sign-off (P15) are pending before this merges.

### Transitions (ADR-0128)

Built: Cut, Fade (also through a colour), Move with its fade-outs, Swipe and Slide on the main canvas and further canvases, in and outside studio mode, with the main canvas's sound following the picture. Per-scene overrides, the T-bar and Rehearse (ADR-0135 phases 3a, 3b and 3c, items 7 and 8) are built. Stinger (ADR-0140, item 9) is built in the studio; its UI is not yet.

1. **Kinds.** A program change of `update` (a scene switch, a studio-mode transition, a follower following) looks at the collection's `transition()`. Move (the default) is the glide of ADR-0100 plus the fade-out of what leaves (1a). Cut sends the new scene's layers without a glide. A Fade, Swipe or Slide with a duration above 0 runs the steps below; one of 0 ms is a cut.
1b. **Which transition** (ADR-0135 decision 8). The kind and duration come from, in order: a one-off (`set_transition_once`), the incoming scene's `transition_in` (the main canvas's new program scene, else the first further canvas's), then the collection: its `kind` while the main canvas is in studio mode (it has a preview scene), its `direct` outside it (ADR-0153; `kind_for`), further canvases following the main canvas's mode; the Rehearse plan and the T-bar always use `kind`. Kind and duration are taken part by part; a one-off kind without a duration uses that kind's collection duration, not the scene's. A Move duration sets the glide's duration and drops per-source glides for that change. Test: `crates/rbs-record/tests/studio.rs` `the_incoming_scenes_override_comes_between_a_one_off_and_the_collection`.
1a. **Move.** When the outgoing layers draw a source (globals left out) that the new program scene does not draw (`visible_sources_cued`) and the glide's duration is above 0, a Move runs as a transition without an incoming compositor: the `outgoing` entry is `OutgoingScene::without_incoming` with only the leaving sources, it starts at once (`Running`, its `started` event in the same update), and the program compositor gets the new scene's layers with their glide (`glide_layers`), then the leaving layers with a linear `Fade` from their opacity to 0 over the glide's duration, then the globals. It ends, settles and shapes the sound as below. When nothing leaves, a Move is the glide alone: no transition, no binding, as before.
2. **Waiting.** The canvas gets an entry in the binder's `outgoing` map (the sources the program compositor drew and, on the main canvas, the sources the mixer heard), so the binder binds a compositor `(canvas, Role::Incoming)` drawing the new program scene and keeps the outgoing sources linked and playing. The studio adopts that node with a ready counter (`CompositorNode::with_ready`). The program compositor keeps the outgoing layers.
3. **Running.** When the incoming compositor's ready counter moves, or after `READY_WAIT` (250 ms on the master clock), the program compositor gets the outgoing layers plus, on top, one full-canvas layer of the incoming compositor, `opaque`, drawn by kind with the transition's duration and easing from that time:
   - **Fade:** a `Fade` from 0. **Through a colour** (`via`): first a full-canvas layer whose border is as wide as the canvas (`LayerShape::Box`, so the border covers the whole box, `crates/rbs-gpu/tests/style.rs` `a_border_half_the_box_wide_covers_the_whole_box`) in the colour, fading in over the first half, then the incoming layer fading in over the second half. No colour node: ADR-0128 decision 6 named a colour source; one styled layer of the incoming compositor gives the same picture with nothing added to the graph.
   - **Swipe:** a `Slide` of the incoming layer from one canvas width (Left, Right) or height (Up, Down) off the edge opposite the direction to its place; the outgoing layers stay.
   - **Slide:** the same, and every outgoing layer slides out by the opposite offset.
   Globals stay above it.
4. **Ending, in two steps.** After the duration the program compositor gets the new scene's own layers. Once its parameter channel reads empty (`ParamSender::pending` false), or after `READY_WAIT`, `service` marks the transition done and `take_transition_done()` returns true once; the host then calls `settle_transitions(sources)`, which updates without the `outgoing` entry and so removes the incoming compositor and unlinks what only the outgoing scene used.
5. **One at a time.** A program change while a transition runs on that canvas replaces it: the new one starts from the layers on air, and the incoming compositor is reused with its counter reset to 0. `end_transitions()` ends every transition at once (the host calls it when studio mode ends); the next update binds without them. A collection switch and `shutdown` drop them with the studio. `update_same_picture` is `update` for a collection whose program scenes show the same picture under other scene ids (the desktop app's held program copy taken or given back, ADR-0063 and the ADR-0151 follow-up): a program change in it starts no transition and no glide.
5a. **Sound follows the picture** (decision 10, main canvas). The transition remembers the mixer's levels before the change and the sources the new program scene is heard with (`visible_sources_cued`). While it waits, every strip keeps its outgoing level and a source only in the incoming scene is silent (its strip muted). When it runs, every strip gets its incoming level, silent for a source only in the outgoing scene, with `ramp_override` set to the picture's start and duration, so the mixer moves each fader with the picture: outgoing-only sources go to silence, incoming-only ones rise from silence (also when they join), shared ones move between their two levels (ADR-0091). From the end on, the same levels without the override (the ramps run on). A fader, mute or master change during the transition goes through `set_mix` and keeps this shape; a strip whose target changes takes a new ramp over what is left of the window. Each phase change sends the strips once; nothing is sent per frame.
6. **Observing.** `transition_state(canvas)` is `None`, `Waiting`, `Running` or `Ending` (`TransitionState`, `#[non_exhaustive]`). `transition_wait(now)` is how long the control thread may sleep before a step; `None` while no transition runs, so no timer is armed (P13). With no transition nothing of this exists: no node, layer, link or per-frame check.
7. **T-bar** (ADR-0135 decisions 1 to 7, main canvas). `transition_progress(canvas, t, sources) -> Result<ManualStep, RecordError>` drives a manual transition; `ManualStep` is `Idle` (nothing runs, as after `t = 0` with none), `Holding` or `Take`. The first `t > 0` ends a timed transition at once and starts a manual one towards the canvas's preview scene: the binder entry is `OutgoingScene::towards(preview)`, so the incoming compositor draws the preview scene and the mixer also hears its `visible_sources_cued`, while the program scene stays bound. It waits for the ready gate as item 2, then holds: each new `t` sends the frozen layers of the timed form at `t` (`frozen_blend`: Fade and through a colour as item 3, Swipe and Slide at offset `e(t)`, Move as a crossfade) and sets each strip to the linear blend of its outgoing and incoming gains at `e(t)` with the default mixer ramp (a muted side counts as 0). A held manual transition never ends by time (`transition_wait` is `None` for it). `t = 1` answers `Take`: the owner changes the program; the next `update` sees `manual_take`, so the change runs as a Cut and the manual entry, now `OutgoingScene::with_program`, goes to `Ending` as item 4 and pushes `Ended` with `manual` and the time since `Started`. `cancel_manual(canvas)` (`t = 0`, or before another program change or preview change) sends the outgoing layers and levels back and ends with `to == from`. `manual_transition(canvas)` tells whether one runs. Events: `Started` (duration 0, `manual`), `Holding` with `progress` at most once per service step, `Ended`.
8. **Rehearse** (ADR-0135 decisions 11 to 13, main canvas). `rehearse(sources) -> Result<bool, RecordError>` plays, on the Preview picture only, what a `canvas.transition` into the preview scene would run now (`rehearsal_plan`: the scene's override, then the collection; Move as a crossfade of the glide's duration). It adds a `CompositorNode` of the preview picture's size (not the canvas's) linked from the program compositor and the preview compositor (a compositor output fans out), moves the preview picture's input to it, and sends it two timed layers (`rehearsal_layers`): the program picture, and over it the preview picture with the `Fade` or `Slide` of the timed form, offsets in the rehearsal's size; through a colour adds the colour layer between. Both pictures contain the global scene, so in a rehearsed Swipe or Slide the globals move with them. It answers `false` and changes nothing outside studio mode, without a preview picture (no UI, or a platform without one), while a transition waits, runs, ends or holds on the canvas, during a rehearsal, and for Cut or a duration of 0. `rehearsal_wait(now)` is how long the control thread may sleep before it ends (`None` without one, P13); once `rehearsal_done()`, the owner calls `end_rehearsal(sources)`, which links the picture back to the preview compositor and removes the node after an update. A new program or preview scene in an update, a T-bar move and `end_transitions` end it at once. Program, outputs, the mixer and `transition.state` never change, and no sound is rehearsed. `rehearsing()` tells whether one plays. Tests: `crates/rbs-record/tests/studio.rs` `a_rehearsal_plays_on_the_preview_picture_only_and_ends` (Windows and macOS, where the preview picture runs) and `without_a_preview_picture_nothing_is_rehearsed`; unit test `a_rehearsal_blends_the_two_pictures_in_its_own_size`.

9. **Stinger** (ADR-0140 decisions 4 to 8, `studio/stinger.rs`). ### Pictures (ADR-0125)

- **Adding a picture.** `Studio::add_picture(collection, sources, canvas, PreviewDesc) -> Result<(PictureId, Updated), RecordError>` links a new preview node to the program compositor of a running canvas (the main canvas or a further one), at the description's size; any number of pictures may show one canvas. A projector's picture is one (ADR-0125 decision 2). `picture(id)` is its endpoint once it runs, `picture_stats(id)` its counters, `pictures()` the ids in the order they were added. `UnknownCanvas` when the canvas does not run.
- **Not kept on failure.** The update runs at once. When it fails, or the picture's node does not join, the picture is taken back and the call fails (`Start` with node `"picture"` for a node that did not join); nothing is kept for a later update, unlike `set_canvas_picture`.
- **Not essential.** A later permanent fault of the node sets `picture_failure(id)` and nothing else: the studio and its outputs run on, and the owner removes the picture.
- **Removing.** `remove_picture(collection, sources, id)` removes the node and its link with an update; the node leaves the engine after the apply. An id that is not there changes nothing. `deactivate_canvas` removes the canvas's pictures with it.
- **Cost.** No picture: nothing of them exists (P13). A further canvas composes while it has a picture of either kind or an output (scheduling follows the graph: a compositor without a consumer is not planned). Pictures are not saved and do not survive a restart: the owner adds them again (ADR-0125 decision 3).

### Several outputs (ADR-0047)

- **Partitions by role.** Each output's node gets its own output partition, so a transport that stalls or reconnects stalls only its own queue. Each video encoder chain (an export on `RENDER` plus a video encoder) gets a video encoder partition, each audio encoder an audio encoder partition. Outputs and encoder chains are two pools (ADR-0127 decision 2), so outputs that share one encoder do not each hold encoder partitions: output slot `s` uses `output` (slot 0), `second`, `more` (slots 1 to 3) or `pool.outputs[s - 4]`; video and audio chain `i` use the encoder partitions of slot `i` for `i < 4` and `pool.video_encoders[i - 4]` / `pool.audio_encoders[i - 4]` above. Each is taken separately: the first one that is free and has no instance whose take is still pending (its swap or drain in flight), so a stop followed at once by a start never cancels the stopped output's drain. An output slot is free while fewer than `MAX_OUTPUTS` run, a chain slot while fewer than `MAX_CHAINS` chains of that kind run (else `NoEncoderSlot`), once pending takes are done; until then `start_output` fails with `OutputFinishing` (try again after `service`). `Studio::start` adds every partition of both pools: 18 more than four slots needed, about 3 KB of heap each and no thread (measured 2026-11-16, counting allocator: 54 KB for the 18); they get a worker only while something is scheduled on them (P13).
- **A destination that joins a hold** (ADR-0127 decision 13). A holdable output that starts on a chain whose hold switch is on starts held (its `output_hold` picture is the chain's, `held_at` its start), and `release_hold` of any stream on that chain releases it too. A shared hold switch for own-encoding chains of one stream (`OutputSettings` shared-hold input) comes with the host step.
- **Sharing.** A running video encoder is reused when the new output has video and its resolved output size equals the size that encoder was opened with, its `VideoSettings` equal those it was opened with, and its adapter is a candidate of the new output's selection (the output's registry holds it and it meets the codec, memory and pixel-format requirement; a registry narrowed to another encoder, the user's explicit choice, gets its own). The resolved size is `OutputSettings::size`, or that output's canvas size when `size` is `None`. A different size is a separate export and a separate encoder, even when every video setting matches; both exports read that canvas, and neither reads the other's planes. A candidate whose declared `codec.video.width` or `codec.video.height` excludes the resolved size is skipped; if none remain, the start is `NoAdapter`. The audio encoder likewise with `AudioSettings` (audio has no pixel size). Video and audio are decided independently. A shared encoder's `packets` pin feeds both outputs, each link with its own packet queue; a shared encoder takes no request that would change one output's stream only (any such future feature, such as a per-output bitrate change, ends the sharing first). Different settings get a chain of their own: a second export and a second encoder session (selection moves on to the next candidate when a hardware encoder refuses another session). **One readback per output shape (perf audit R-01).** The new chain's export uses the virtual camera's `DownloadShare` when the camera is sending that canvas at that resolved size in NV12, Rec. 709 limited, with closed shadows; otherwise the `DownloadShare` of a running system-memory chain of the same canvas, resolved output size, pixel format (`VideoSettings::format`), colour and open-shadows grade; otherwise a new one (`VideoExportNode::with_share`). A native chain is not a source. Exports of one share convert and read a canvas picture back once and both take that one frame buffer (`Bytes`, no second copy); each keeps its own stamps, pause window, hold, end and counters, so pausing the recording or holding the stream never touches the other. `Progress::frames_joined` counts an output's frames that rode on the other's readback (test `a_recording_and_a_stream_with_different_settings_run_on_their_own_encoders`). Changing `size` does not restart the studio or move scene items; an output already started keeps the size it started with, and the new size is used the next time that output starts.
- **Joining.** An output that joins a running encoder starts at its next keyframe (the packet queue endpoint's rule), at most a keyframe interval after `start_output`; the encoder is not asked for an early keyframe. Its audio before the first video packet is held and trimmed by the output's aligned start (I/O nodes spec, `START_HOLD`); one stopped before its first keyframe has no video (and possibly no media at all).
- **Counters.** `output_progress(id)` counts from the output's start request: the shared chain's counters at that moment are its base. Packets the join's resync dropped (up to one GOP) are counted by the queue, not as loss.

`Recording::start` is a studio without a preview, in a strict mode, with the recording's nodes added before the first apply: a binder problem that leaves out a source of the program scene fails the start (`Problems`) and a source the recording schedules (video and audio) that cannot be instantiated fails it (`Source`), both before any device opens (test `a_video_source_that_cannot_be_instantiated_fails_the_start_before_any_device_opens`). `finish` is `Studio::shutdown`; its `shutdown_errors` hold the draining apply's errors and the engine shutdown's.

### Replay buffer (ADR-0088)

`rbs_nodes::replay::PacketRing` is the buffer's data structure, `ReplayRingNode` feeds it, `Studio::arm_replay` runs it and the studio host (rbs-app) arms it, saves clips and publishes its state. The UI follows in a later change.

- Holds **encoded packets** (`RingEntry`: stream index plus `EncodedPacket`, payload shared as `Bytes`), in arrival order. Limits: `length`, `memory_limit` and `capacity` (packets, reserved in `PacketRing::new`).
- `push` is hot: it never grows storage and evicts the **oldest** entries while a limit is exceeded; the newest packet is never dropped. Evicted entries go to a caller-reserved vector for a reaper thread; if it has no room the entry is freed in place and counted (`inline_frees`).
- `held()` reports the usable past (oldest video keyframe to newest packet), bytes, packets and `limited_by: Some(Memory)` when memory is why it is shorter than `length` ("Holds the last 3:12, limit: memory").
- `rbs_nodes::replay_node::ReplayRingNode` (`org.rbs.replay-ring`, kind `Output`, inputs `s0` video, `s1` audio, ...) feeds the ring from `process`. Its `ReplayHandle` (control thread) offers `status()` (held, bytes, packets, `memory_limited`, `inline_frees`, `clips_cut`), `request_clip(seconds)` (one at a time, else `Busy`) and `take_clip()`. The node takes no lock: the request is an atomic, the clip is cut into storage reserved at arm time and returned by `try_send` on a bounded channel, and evicted packets go to a reaper thread in batches of 32 through bounded channels with recycled batches. The reaper blocks in `recv` (no polling) and ends when `release` closes the channel; `release` frees the buffered past on the control thread. A node exists only while armed.
- `Studio::arm_replay(adapters, &ReplaySettings)` adds the buffer's own video export and encoder, an audio encoder (with `audio`; PCM and FLAC pass `bit_depth` the same way as a recording) and a `ReplayRingNode` on the replay partitions (`Partitions::replay`: `REPLAY_VIDEO_ENCODER`, `REPLAY_AUDIO_ENCODER`, `REPLAY_RING`; workers start only while armed, so a disarmed buffer costs nothing). The replay export stays at the canvas size: ADR-0102's output size does not apply to it. It is not one of the `MAX_OUTPUTS` outputs. `ReplayArmed` when armed already; `Start` (everything removed again) when a node does not start. `replay_status()`, `request_replay_clip(seconds)` and `take_replay_clip()` forward to the handle. The ring publishes held time, bytes and packet count when it stores a packet or cuts a clip; an empty tick leaves them, because held time is the span of stored timestamps, not the clock. `disarm_replay()` removes the nodes in one apply and frees the buffered past. First version: the buffer always opens its own encoder sessions; sharing the encoders of a running output with equal settings (ADR-0088 decision 2) and the `replay.*` control API follow.
- `write_clip(adapters, &Clip, streams, url, container)` (`Studio::replay_streams()` gives the streams) rebases the clip and writes it through a throwaway muxer and file transport, without re-encoding; it blocks until the file is closed (at most `CLIP_WRITE_TIMEOUT` for a full queue or a slow close), so it runs on a worker thread of the composition root, never on the control thread or a real-time path. It touches no output, the buffer or the graph. The muxer's own chunking makes the file valid even if the app dies during the write (ADR-0088 decision 4).
- `cut(seconds, out)` starts at the latest video keyframe at or before `newest - seconds` (the oldest keyframe if the ring is shorter; the whole ring for `None`), so a clip is never shorter than asked unless the ring is. Audio is cut at the same instant. Without a video keyframe it fails with `ClipError::NoKeyframe`. `Clip::rebased` shifts timestamps so the earliest sample is 0; it runs on the writer task.
- **Host layer** (rbs-app `studio_host/replay.rs`). The settings are `replay.toml` (`ReplaySetup`: length 10 s to 30 min, memory 64 MiB to 8 GiB, audio, folder, arm on start), told to the host with `SetReplay`; a changed setting applies the next time the buffer arms, and `replay.status` shows the running values while armed. The buffer is re-armed after a canvas or collection change (the studio is made again); if that fails, `last_error` says why. One clip is cut at a time (`conflict`), and at most two clip files are written at once; a writer is a short-lived thread, so capture and every output keep running. A clip is `RBS Replay <date time>.<ext>` in the folder, never replacing a file. While armed the host looks at the buffer once a second (state) and every 20 ms while a clip is requested; there is no timer while disarmed (ADR-0013).
- **State out.** `replay.changed` (control API 1.27) is published when the state changes: about once a second while armed (`bytes` and `held_secs` move), never while disarmed and unchanged. `last_saved` is an absolute path and `last_error` free text, both visible to `read` tokens like a source's `detail`.
- **Known deviations from ADR-0088.** The buffer always opens its own export and encoder session (no sharing with a running recording yet, decision 2), so an armed buffer beside a recording costs a second encode per frame. The API has no separate `replay.saved` event: completion is `saving` going false with a new `last_saved` or `last_error`. Retroactive recording (`record.start {include_buffer}`) is not built yet. The node's hand-off wakes the reaper with one non-blocking channel send per 32 evicted packets, which can take a short internal lock; P15 numbers for the whole armed buffer with 0, 1 and 2 other outputs and for a 30 minute cut are still to be measured.

### Pause and hold (ADR-0124; partly built)

Built (phase 1, steps 2 to 4): `OutputSettings::pausable`, the window record (`rbs_core::pause`), the export and audio encoder gates, the output node cut for shared encoders (`rbs_nodes::output_cut`), and `Studio::pause_output` / `resume_output` / `output_pause` with the `Progress` fields. Separate files follow the window when started with `IsoVideoStart::pause_with` naming the recording, or, for separate audio files, which open before their recording, with `IsoAudioStart::pause` holding a `SharedPause` made up front that the recording is then started with (`OutputSettings::shared_pause`): their exports and audio encoders are built with the same window, their video encoders take a keyframe at each join, and image tracks (`ImageTrackNode::with_pause`) take a change made while paused at the join, stamped in file time. Separate files started without it make a pause `PauseRefused::SeparateFiles`. A recording's windows: one for the recording (its state, its separate files), one per chain it built (written only while it is that chain's only user; a chain ever paused is not shared), and an `OutputCut` on its output node (written for the streams it shares, with a mask of those streams). Each pause is planned per chain: owned chains are cut before their encoders, shared ones at the output node, which needs the shared video encoder's adapter to declare `codec.reconfigure: keyframe` (else `PauseRefused::KeyframeUnsupported`); on resume the shared encoder gets a keyframe request at `resume_at` in master time, an owned one at the join in file time. Every output video encoder is built with keyframe requests (the accepted cost of decision 8). The manifest's pauses and markers while paused are built (protocol 1.63, "Recording manifest"). The desktop app starts every recording `pausable` (its separate files with it), and the control API's `record.pause`, `record.resume` and `record.pause_toggle` (protocol 1.61) drive it; `record.state` carries `paused`, `paused_ms` and `recorded_ms`, published on each pause and resume. The window's Hold stream and Break (registry `stream.hold_toggle`, `output.break_toggle`) are built; they and a hold without a picture use the stream settings' "While on hold" (`hold_picture`, protocol 1.65). A pause that cut a shared audio encoder's packets at the output node writes `join_error_ns`, one audio packet of that encoder (`PauseState::join_error`, about 21.3 ms for AAC at 48 kHz): audio passes again from the first packet at or after the join, so the sound after it may start up to that much late, never early; a pause of the recording's own encoders writes none. The hold scene (2b) is built, below; device runs are pending. Tests: `crates/rbs-record/tests/pause.rs` (separate video and audio files included), `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/stream.rs` (`a_control_api_client_pauses_and_resumes_a_recording`), `crates/rbs-nodes/tests/image_track_gpu.rs` (`a_change_inside_a_pause_*`), `crates/rbs-nodes/tests/pipeline_gpu.rs` (`a_paused_export_*`, `two_pauses_add_up`, `a_pause_cancelled_*`, `an_end_while_paused_*`), `rbs-audio` `gate::tests`, `rbs-core` `pause::tests`.

As built, differing from the planned text below: the window is `{epoch, before, pause_at, resume_at}` behind a sequence lock over fixed fields (`PauseWindow`), with `before` the time left out by earlier pauses (`paused_total` is `before + resume_at - pause_at`). A request's instant is the render tick three ticks after the one in progress (`Cadence::frames(0, fps)`), so at least two render periods ahead. `pause_output` calls `ExportEnd::wake_at(pause_at)`, which lowers `next` with `fetch_min`; the export raises it again by compare-and-swap once the window is behind it. `resume_output` asks the video encoder for a keyframe at the join in file time (`pause_at - before`); a pausable recording's own encoder is always built with keyframe requests, and a session that refuses one only counts it (an owned chain is cut before the encoder, so its file is right either way). `output_pause(id)` returns `PauseState {paused, pause_at, resume_at, paused_ns}`. A chain whose window was ever written (`PauseWindow::version() != 0`) is never shared with a later output. Errors are `PauseRefused::{NotRunning, NotPausable, KeyframeUnsupported, SeparateFiles, Ending, AlreadyPaused, NotPaused}`.

In the window (step 5, owner approval 2026-11-15): `studio_host::Command::PauseRecording` runs `record.pause_toggle` with `canvas: "all"` (pause every running recording, or resume them all when none runs unpaused); every pause or resume, from the window or a control API client, sends `Update::RecordPause`, each canvas's `RecordPause { paused, recorded_ns }` (`None` while it does not record), from which the window moves its clocks on. A refusal is the toast "The recording could not pause or resume: <why>.". The controls are in the [design system](ui-design-system.md) (Go live and Record, Outputs). `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/record.rs` `the_window_pauses_and_resumes_the_recording`.

Planned contract (the parts not built are marked above):

- **`Studio::pause_output(id)` / `resume_output(id)`** on the control thread. A pause is one window record `{pause_at, resume_at, paused_total}` published whole (a seqlock over two fixed slots, Release/Acquire); its instants are render tick times at least two render periods after the request, so `paused_total` is a whole number of frames. A resume before `pause_at` cancels the pause (no keyframe, no manifest row); requests apply in order. Every file of the recording (master, separate video, audio and picture files) uses the same window. Errors: `NotRunning`, `NotPausable` (an output built without `PauseGate`), `AlreadyPaused` / `NotPaused`, `Ending`, `KeyframeUnsupported` (a shared video encoder whose adapter does not declare `codec.reconfigure: keyframe`). Stop while paused finalises at once (`output_tail_done` is true).
- **Pause and end compose.** The export's run-path atomic `next` is the earliest of the end time and the next window instant, lowered by compare-and-swap; only the end path sets `ended` and `ExportEnd::requested`.
- **Where the cut is.** Per chain, decided at each pause: an owned video chain is cut at its export (no canvas in `[pause_at, resume_at)`, later frames stamped `t - paused_total`); an owned audio encoder drops the window's samples (block split by sample count, 5 ms fades in a scratch buffer from `prepare`), later blocks stamped earlier by the dropped duration so the encoder sees no gap (output ports invariant 8); an image track writes nothing while paused and retakes a changed picture at the join. A shared encoder is cut at the recording's output node on packets (video in decode order, resume at the keyframe requested for the first frame at or after `resume_at`, dts kept monotonic; audio from the first packet at or after `resume_at`). A keyframe is requested at each join when the adapter declares the capability.
- **Sharing.** A chain with an open window or `paused_total > 0` is never shared with an output started later (the same filter that refuses an ended export).
- **Gates at build time.** The audio encoder node and the output node take `PauseGate` for recordings and their separate files, `NoGate` (nothing compiled) for the replay buffer and the virtual camera; a stream's audio encoder node takes `HoldGate`. The export follows its pause window and its hold only on its slow path (`next` lowered), so neither changes its run path.
- **`Progress`** gains `paused` (bool), `paused_ns` (total) and `recorded_ns` (media time written).
- **Hold (phase 2a built in `rbs-record`).** `OutputSettings::holdable` builds a stream's new chains with a hold: an `ExportHold` (a `HoldSwitch` plus a black frame made on the first black hold) on the export and a `HoldGate` on the audio encoder node. A `pausable` recording's new chains get one too (`PauseGate::with_hold`), so a stream that later shares them can hold (decision 14). `Studio::hold_output(id, HoldPicture::{Freeze, Black})` turns the chains' switches on and lowers the export's `next`; it returns when the hold began. From the next render tick the export reads nothing back: on the hold's first tick it sends the newest readback as on the run path and pins it as the frozen picture (taken out of the ring, no copy); then each tick it sends the hold picture (frozen, or the black frame) with the previous tick's time and a new sequence, so timestamps run on with one frame per tick and no gap or repeat on either edge (a canvas leaves the export one tick after it was drawn). The audio encoder node submits silence with each block's own timestamp. Calling `hold_output` again with the other picture changes it (the frozen frame stays pinned while black is sent). `release_hold(id)` turns the switches off: the export's next tick sends the hold picture once more if no readback is ready, raises `next` again by compare-and-swap and goes back to the program. The hold is on the chain: every holdable output sharing it is held and released with it. An output that is not holdable (a recording) sharing it must be paused, its pause already begun, or the hold is `HoldRefused::SharedWithRecording`; while the hold is on, `resume_output` of that recording is `PauseRefused::Held`. Other refusals: `NotRunning`, `NotHoldable` (not started holdable, or a chain built without a hold), `PictureUnsupported` (no black frame for the export's pixel format), `Ending`, `AlreadyHeld`, `NotHeld`. `output_hold(id)` gives `HoldState { picture, held_at, held_ns }`; `Progress::held` and `held_ns` count holds (always false and 0 for an output that is not holdable). The desktop app starts every stream `holdable`. Its host pauses a sharing recording first, keeps the hold waiting until that pause has begun (looked at on each service pass), refuses to resume that recording while the hold is on or waits, and resumes only a recording its hold paused (decision 14); Break holds every stream and pauses every recording (decision 15). The control API drives both (protocol 1.64, `control-api.md`); `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/stream.rs` `a_control_api_client_holds_a_stream_and_takes_a_break`. `Scene` (phase 2b) adds a compositor for the hold scene on `RENDER` only while holding. Tests: `crates/rbs-record/tests/hold.rs`, `rbs-nodes` `export::tests::the_black_hold_picture_*`, `rbs-audio` `gate::tests::a_held_*` and `a_recording_chain_held_*`, `rbs-core` `pause::tests::a_hold_switch_*`.
- **Manifest** (ADR-0089 format 1): every existing time is file time with the pauses removed (`duration_ns`, `start_offset_ns`, image `at_ns`, marker `t_ns`, `pressed_ns`), so a reader that knows nothing of pauses stays right. Added: `paused_ns`, and `[[pauses]]` with `at_file_ns`, `from_ns`, `to_ns` and, for a shared cut, `join_error_ns`. A marker pressed while paused sits on that pause's join with `while_paused = true`; a marker lead never reaches back past the join before its press. The editor project writes a marker per pause when the recording setting asks for it (on by default).

#### Hold scene (phase 2b; planned, owner approved the window design 2026-11-16)

A hold can show a scene of the collection instead of the last frame or black, with that scene's sound or silence (ADR-0124 decision 13). Nothing of it exists while no stream holds with a scene (P13). Review: round 1 (independent AI session, 2026-11-16) changes requested, 8 major and 5 minor; round 2 approve with changes (1 major, 4 minor), resolved in this text. Built so far: `HoldPicture::Scene` (the export sends the frozen picture for it until the scene path is built) and host-only pins (media graph spec); the binder's `Role::Hold` compositor and hold mixer (`sync_held`, scene model spec); the export's scene path (io-nodes spec); the audio encoder's hold input and `HoldGate`'s scene timeline (below); the studio's scene picture and fallback (below).

- **Types and layering.** `rbs_core::pause::HoldPicture` gains a payload-free `Scene` (switch code 3) and becomes `#[non_exhaustive]` (a breaking change of `rbs-core`, not of any protocol; the in-tree matches are updated). The scene and sound live in `rbs-record`: `Studio::hold_output(id, Hold)` with `Hold::Picture(HoldPicture)` (phase 2a) or `Hold::Scene { scene: SceneId, sound: HoldSound }`, `HoldSound::{Silence, Scene}`.
- **One hold scene per canvas.** The binder takes at most one hold scene per canvas (`BindRequest::hold: BTreeMap<CanvasId, HoldScene { scene, sound }>`, empty by default). A second stream of the same canvas that holds with another scene or sound while one is held is refused (`HoldRefused::OtherSceneHeld`); with the same scene it shares the compositor and mixer. `HoldRefused::UnknownScene` for a scene not in `showable_scenes(canvas)`.
- **Binder.** `Role::Hold`. For `(canvas, Hold)` it binds a compositor like the preview one: layers from the scene's visible sources, no warm sources. The hold scene's visible temporal sources (media, browser) join `running` (as program, preview and cued scenes do), so they play while held. A source in the program or preview scene too keeps its one instance. With `sound: Scene` it binds a hold mixer (`SceneBinder::mixer_of(canvas, Role::Hold)`, type `org.rbs.audio.mixer`) linked from each audio source visible in the hold scene as the program mixer is (ADR-0039, ADR-0106). A picture-and-sound source heard by both mixers feeds both from its one sound node (ADR-0106). The studio builds the hold mixer's strips from `BoundMixer::inputs` at the sources' levels (the scene's own levels when it keeps them, ADR-0091) and sends it every volume and mute change as it does the program mixer (ADR-0081). The hold mixer has no monitor output and no meters. Removing the request retires both and, through the plan, sources no other role keeps.
- **Graph rule.** `rbs_graph::Pin` gains `host_only: bool` (`Pin::host_only(name, kind)`). A `VideoFilter` may have a second video input and an `Encoder` a second audio input only when that pin is host-only; the plugin loader refuses a third-party descriptor with a host-only pin. The export and the audio encoder declare `hold` that way. `node.rs` and its tests change with it; media-graph.md says so.
- **Export (hot path).** While the switch says `Scene`, the hold path submits the canvas arriving on `hold` to `YuvDownload` each tick, as the run path submits `canvas`; nothing from `canvas` is submitted. The export keeps, beside each ring slot it submits to, which pin the canvas came from (no change to `rbs-gpu`), so a program readback still in flight when the hold began is dropped, and so is a scene readback at release. Every frame sent on the hold path, frozen or scene, is stamped with the previous tick's time and a new sequence (phase 2a's rule), so timestamps stay strictly increasing with no gap across both edges: until the first scene readback is ready the frozen frame goes out, then the newest scene readback, and a tick whose readback is late repeats the last frame sent. A test drives a readback two ticks late at each edge and checks the timestamps strictly increase. The frozen frame's pinned buffer is released at the first scene frame. On release the export does as in phase 2a with the last scene frame as `Held::picture` until the first program readback (submitted from the release tick) is ready.
- **Audio encoder (hot path).** `AudioGate::pass` takes `hold: Option<&AudioBlock>` (always `None` for `NoGate` and for gates of chains without a hold link). The node keeps a pre-allocated two-block slot for `hold`: both mixers tick on `AUDIO` on one cadence, so blocks pair by equal timestamp; a `hold` block older than the `in` block is dropped, a missing one means silence for that block. `HoldGate` and `PauseGate::with_hold` (a chain shared with a recording) both pass the paired `hold` block in place of silence while held with `Scene`. The slot is read only inside the held branch. **As built (step 3b, 2026-11-16):** the timestamp-paired slot was replaced, because each audio queue passes one block a tick and rings the encoder's doorbell on each write, so the scene's block of a time can reach the encoder a tick after the program's block of that time and pairing by equal time would fall one block behind. `AudioGate` keeps `pass(block, submit)` and gains `pass_hold(block, submit)` with a `HOLDS` constant (`false` compiles the encoder's read of its "hold" input away; only `HoldGate` sets it). Held with `Scene`, `HoldGate` keeps one cursor, the time where the next part starts: the program's block is not sent; the scene's blocks go out as they arrive from the cursor on (a block, or the part of one, before it is dropped); a program block that starts past the cursor means the scene is more than a block behind or silent, and silence fills up to it. The first program block of the scene opens the cursor, the first program block after it (release or another picture) fills up to it and closes it, so there is no gap or overlap on either edge and the scene's sound runs at most one block (10 ms) behind; with no lag the scene starts after one block of silence. No copy and no slot: nothing is reserved beyond the gate's silence. `PauseGate::with_hold` (a chain a recording shares) sent silence for a scene hold at this step; it learns the scene's sound later (studio step 2, below). The "hold" input is `encoder_hold_descriptor` (`EncoderNode::with_hold`). Tests: `gate.rs` (either lag, a scene that falls silent, freeze to scene to black and release, scene blocks ignored while not a scene hold, no allocation).
- **Apply timing and fallback.** `hold_output` with a scene binds, adopts and applies; the switch goes to `Scene` at once and the export keeps the frozen frame until scene canvases arrive. When no scene canvas arrives within 2 s of master time (a `prepare` failure, ADR P7), or the scene is removed or the collection switched during the hold, the studio sets the switch to `Freeze` and releases the scene parts; `HoldState` and `Studio::output_hold` report `fallback: true` with the reason. The sound during a fallback is silence (the switch is `Freeze`, no `hold` block is read); a hold mixer that fails while the compositor runs falls back to silence alone, with its reason. `hold` blocks that arrive between the apply and the switch are dropped. **As built (studio step 1, picture, 2026-11-16):** `Studio::hold_output_scene(id, scene, sound, sources)` (the sources open the scene's media and browser sources) and `Studio::release_hold_scene(id, sources)` (releases, then syncs at once); `hold_output(id, picture)` stays for `Freeze` and `Black` and refuses `Scene` without a bound scene (`HoldRefused::NoScene`). Other refusals: `UnknownScene`, `SceneNotShowable` (not in `showable_scenes(canvas)`), `OtherSceneHeld`, `NotBound` (bind or apply failed; nothing stays). The studio keeps one `HoldRun` per canvas; its compositor is adopted when the binder bounds `(canvas, Role::Hold)` and linked to the "hold" input of every holdable export of the canvas; its layers are sent with the program's. `ExportHold::scene_frames` counts scene frames read back; `Studio::service` falls a run back (`HoldFallback::NoPicture`) when it has not grown `HOLD_SCENE_TIMEOUT` (2 s) after the hold began, or `Failed` when the compositor fails; an update whose collection no longer shows the scene on the canvas gives `SceneGone`. Tests: `rbs-record/tests/hold.rs` (`a_stream_held_with_a_scene_sends_it_and_comes_back_without_a_gap`: no fallback past 2 s, so scene frames arrived, and the stream's timestamps run on with no gap; `a_hold_scene_that_leaves_the_collection_falls_back_to_the_frozen_frame`). **As built (studio step 2, sound, 2026-11-16):** with `HoldSound::Scene` and audio on, the run asks the binder for the hold mixer; the studio adopts a `MixerNode` at the bound node (in the binder's audio partition) and links its "main" output to the "hold" input of every audio encoder of the canvas's streams that has one (a stream's own chain, `HoldGate`). ### Virtual camera (ADR-0066)

- **Not an output.** The camera is none of the `MAX_OUTPUTS` outputs: it has no encoder or output partition and runs alongside recordings and streams without touching them. It shows the program compositor's canvas, whatever studio mode shows (ADR-0066 decision 5).
- **Start.** `start_virtual_camera` fails with `InvalidConfig` while the camera already sends. It opens the session (`factory.open` with NV12, system memory, the settings' size and rate) on the calling control thread, so `NotInstalled` and the adapter's other refusals come back before anything is added. Then a `VirtualCameraNode` for the canvas size is added Active on `RENDER`, linked from the program compositor's "canvas", adopted and applied on a copy of the graph. The node takes `with_share` of a running system-memory export of this canvas whose output size, NV12, Rec. 709 limited and closed shadows match the camera, or a new share a later export of that shape joins (audit F-04). A native export is not a source of that share. An adoption or apply refusal is `Os { code: 0, .. }`, and the node is taken back (the session drops with it, on this thread).
- **Running.** The node keeps the program compositor scheduled, so the render worker and the program's video sources run while the camera sends, even with no preview and no output. The node is not essential: its failure is kept in `VirtualCameraState::failure` (cleared when it runs again), never in `failure()` or an output's failure.
- **Stop.** `stop_virtual_camera` removes the node with a plain apply, then takes it back. If the render partition still runs it, the take is queued in the pending takes, and `service` completes it. Either way the node, and with it the session whose drop joins the adapter's sender thread, is dropped on the control thread, never on `RENDER`. `shutdown` stops the camera first. Stopping never uninstalls the camera.

`Recording` passes `start_virtual_camera`, `stop_virtual_camera` and `virtual_camera` through to its studio; `finish` stops the camera with the rest.

### Monitor output (ADR-0097 F1)

- **Strips.** Every mixer strip carries its source's `SourceAudio::monitor` (`Off`, `Only`, `Also`) and whether it is soloed; `update`, `set_mix` and `set_solo` send them with the levels. The mixer does monitor work only while its `monitor` pin is read by a node with an instance, so these values cost nothing without a monitor, and `Only` stays in the mix (rbs-audio spec, "Monitor").
- **Host owns the device.** `monitor_wanted` is true when a strip of the main canvas's mixer is monitored or soloed. The host opens the monitor device (an `AudioOutput` in Standby for the graph's format) when it turns true and calls `start_monitor`; it calls `stop_monitor` when it turns false, and it retries a failed device with back-off (ADR-0097 decisions 4 and 7). The studio never opens or retries a device.
- **Start.** `start_monitor` fails with `MonitorPlaying` while a monitor plays and `NotBound("mixer")` without audio. Otherwise it adds an `OutputNode` (target `DEFAULT_OUTPUT_TARGET`) Active on the audio partition, links the mixer's `monitor` pin to its `in`, adopts and applies on a copy of the graph. The mixer instance is kept, so strip ramps survive and the stream does not glitch. An apply refusal takes the node back (the device drops with it on this thread). A device that cannot start fails the node's `prepare`: the monitor stays with `MonitorState::failure` set and plays nothing, and the mixer does not count it as a reader.
- **Running.** The node is not essential: its failure is kept in `MonitorState::failure` (cleared when it runs again), never in `failure()` or an output's failure. `MonitorState::latency` is the graph latency plus the ring target plus the device's latency. `set_monitor_volume` ramps (`OUTPUT_VOLUME_RAMP`), clamped to `0..=MAX_OUTPUT_VOLUME`.
- **Stop.** `stop_monitor` removes the node with a plain apply and takes it back, through the pending takes if the audio partition still runs it; the device is stopped and dropped on the control thread. `shutdown` stops the monitor.

### Loudness meter (ADR-0092 stage 1)

- **Start.** `start_loudness` adds a `LoudnessTapNode` Active on the audio partition and links the main canvas mixer's `main` output to it, beside the meter sink and the encoders, then applies. The mixer instance is kept and nothing it feeds changes, so the stream and recordings do not dip. It answers `Ok(true)` when it started, `Ok(false)` when it was already running (the measurement goes on), and `NotBound("mixer")` without audio; a refused apply takes the node back.
- **Tap point.** The mixer's `main` output, after the master volume and mute: what the encoders get while the master has no filter chain. When a master chain exists (ADR-0092 decision 2, a later step), the tap moves to the chain's tail.
- **Reading.** `loudness()` is `Some(Loudness)` while it runs (rbs-audio spec, "Loudness meter"); `reset_loudness` asks the tap to restart the measurement at its next block and is false while it is off.
- **Readout.** `loudness_readout()` gives the running meter's shared values (`Arc<LoudnessReadout>`, `None` while off) so the desktop app's Mixer reads them at its own rate; a new run is a new handle.
- **Stop.** `stop_loudness` removes the node with a plain apply and takes it back like the monitor. `shutdown` stops it. While it is off the studio has no loudness node (ADR-0013).

### Sync offset measurement (ADR-0126; studio calls and estimator built, device memory and control API planned)

#### Studio calls

```rust
pub const MEASURE_WINDOW: Duration;   // 10 s
pub const MEASURE_CLAPS: usize;       // 5
impl Studio {
    pub fn start_sync_measure(&mut self, source: SourceId, reference: SourceId) -> Result<u64, RecordError>;
    pub fn sync_measure(&mut self) -> Option<SyncMeasureStatus>;        // call from the service loop
    pub fn cancel_sync_measure(&mut self) -> Option<SyncMeasureStatus>; // Failed(Cancelled)
    pub fn sync_measure_collected(&self) -> Option<(usize, usize)>;     // ms of sound, pictures logged
}
pub struct SyncMeasureStatus { pub id: u64, pub source: SourceId, pub reference: SourceId, pub state: SyncMeasureState }
pub enum SyncMeasureState { Running { pairs: usize }, Done(SyncMeasureResult), Failed(SyncMeasureFailure) }
pub struct SyncMeasureResult { pub suggestion: Suggestion, pub estimate: Estimate, pub effective_offset_ms: i32 }
pub enum SyncMeasureFailure { TooFewClaps, NoPictureMotion, Inconsistent, OffsetChanged, ChainChanged, SourceClosed, Cancelled }
pub enum SyncMeasureRefused { Busy, NotHeard, NoPicture, NoAudio }   // in RecordError::SyncMeasure
```

- **Start.** Refused with `Busy` while one runs, `NoAudio` without a mixer, `NotHeard` when the bound mixer has no input for `source` (a source the program mix does not hear, or one with no sound), `NoPicture` when `reference` has no chain tail with a video output. Otherwise it adds a `SyncProbeNode` (audio partition) linked from the source's mixer input producer, after its filters and offset and before the fader (the ADR-0095 tap), and a `SyncPictureProbeNode` (render partition) linked from the reference's chain tail, adopts both and applies; on a failure both are taken back and nothing stays. It records the producer and tail, the start time on the engine clock, the stored offset, the **effective** offset, which is the offset the program hears: `h = a + L`, where `a` is what the input applies (`rbs_audio::effective_offset_ns` of `stored - L` with the scene's 200 ms limit) and `L` the latency of the source's scheduled filters (ADR-0130 decision 4; `h` is the clamped stored offset while `L = 0`, and equals the stored offset whenever the compensation is complete) and the input's `resyncs`. Ids count up from 1 per studio. The reference's picture is drawn only while something takes the canvas; in the app the preview does.
- **Status.** `sync_measure` pulls both logs, then ends the measurement with `Failed` when the source left the mix or the reference its picture (`SourceClosed`), when the producer or tail changed or a probe is no longer linked (`ChainChanged`), when `set_audio_offset` changed that source's offset or its input resynchronised (`OffsetChanged`). Otherwise it estimates: a result with at least `MEASURE_CLAPS` pairs, or any result once `MEASURE_WINDOW` passed, ends with `Done` (the suggestion clamped to the input's advance limit and 200 ms); an estimator error after the window ends with that error. Else `Running` with the pairs so far. An ending removes both probes with a plain apply (taken back now or through the pending takes) and returns the final status once; then `None`.
- **Shutdown** ends a running measurement. Nothing is applied by the studio: the caller applies the suggestion through the source's sync offset.
- Tests (`crates/rbs-record/tests/sync_measure.rs`, reference adapters on a `TestClock`): both probes fill while it runs and cancelling removes them (node count back, a new id next); a source the mix does not hear and a reference with no picture are refused, adding nothing; another source's offset change does not end it, the source's own does (`OffsetChanged`) and removes the probes; `slow_` (ignored): ten seconds of steady tones and a test pattern end without a result.

#### Estimator

The pure estimator is `rbs_record::sync_measure` (no I/O, no clock).

```rust
pub struct AudioEnergy { pub time_ns: i64, pub energy: f32 }      // mean square of one 1 ms sub-block
pub struct PictureMotion { pub time_ns: i64, pub motion: f32 }    // one per NEW frame, at its first tick
pub struct Estimate { pub residual_ns: i64, pub pairs: usize, pub spread_ns: i64, pub frame_interval_ns: i64 }
pub enum EstimateError { TooFewClaps, NoPictureMotion, Inconsistent }
pub struct Suggestion { pub measured_ms: i32, pub offset_ms: i32, pub clamped: bool }
pub fn audio_onsets(energy: &[AudioEnergy]) -> Vec<i64>;
pub fn picture_events(frames: &[PictureMotion]) -> Option<(Vec<i64>, i64)>; // events, median interval
pub fn estimate(energy: &[AudioEnergy], frames: &[PictureMotion]) -> Result<Estimate, EstimateError>;
pub fn pair_count(energy: &[AudioEnergy], frames: &[PictureMotion]) -> usize;   // progress
pub fn suggest(effective_offset_ms: i32, e: &Estimate, max_advance_ms: i32, max_delay_ms: i32) -> Suggestion;
```

- **Sound events.** A 1 ms sub-block at least `ONSET_FLOOR_DB` (-50 dBFS) and `ONSET_DB` (12 dB) above the mean energy of the previous `ONSET_HISTORY` (50 ms, at least half of it seen), then `REFRACTORY` (300 ms).
- **Picture events.** Frame `k` is a peak: above frame `k-1`, at least frame `k+1`, at least `PEAK_OVER_MEDIAN` (4) times the window's median motion and `MIN_PEAK_MOTION` (0.004). Within two new frames it falls to at most half, or reaches a local minimum (hands that rebound). Event time `T_k - (T_k - T_(k-1)) / 2`. Within `REFRACTORY` the highest peak is kept.
- **Pairs.** Each sound event goes with the nearest picture event within `SEARCH` (500 ms); a picture event keeps only its nearest sound event. Residual `sound - picture`, positive when the sound is late.
- **Result.** At least `MIN_PAIRS` (3), else `TooFewClaps`; no picture event, `NoPictureMotion`; an interquartile range above the median measured frame interval, `Inconsistent`. Otherwise the mean of the inner half of the sorted residuals.
- **Suggestion.** `effective_offset_ms - round(residual in ms)`, clamped to `-max_advance_ms..=max_delay_ms`; `clamped` when that changed it. A sound 60 ms early suggests about +60, 20 ms late about -20.
- **Accuracy.** One pair is off by up to half a frame interval (the contact is somewhere inside it). Six claps at random phases: within half an interval always, within a quarter in about nine sessions of ten (tests in `sync_measure.rs`: sign both ways, effective offset, clamping, random phases at 24, 30 and 60 fps, rebound, 15 fps, jitter with a missed clap, each error, quiet and steady sound).

### Invariants

1. Engine code names no adapter: `rbs-record` depends on ports, node crates, the scene and the engine only (P1), and selects by capability (P3).
2. Nothing opens before `start` has chosen every adapter: a missing encoder, muxer or transport fails before any device or file is touched.
3. Video packet `pts` is the canvas tick time that produced the frame (on the canvas cadence from time 0); audio packet `pts` starts at the first mixed block's time (tick time minus the graph latency, the time of its first sample) and advances by exactly one codec frame. Both are master-clock time, so streams are in sync by timestamp; the muxer maps them to one origin.
4. When video is set, output stream 0 is video and stream 1 is audio, in the order the output declares them to the muxer. With no video, stream 0 is audio.
5. A recording that is not started holds no thread, device or file; after `finish` every worker has stopped and the transport is finished (P13). After `stop_output` the partitions only that output used have no worker and its transport is finished; video sources are closed unless a preview shows them.
6. `start` and `finish` run on the control thread. Nothing in `rbs-record` runs per frame or per block; the per-frame work is in the nodes ([I/O nodes](io-nodes.md), [audio graph](audio-graph.md)).

### `rbs-cli`

- `rbs-cli record <file.mkv|file.mp4|file.mov> [options]`: `.mkv` is Matroska, `.mp4` fragmented MP4, `.mov` QuickTime written fragmented (ADR-0085; H.264 or HEVC and AAC, as the other two). Options: `--seconds N` (else until Enter; required when stdin is not a terminal, so a script cannot record forever), `--overwrite` (replace an existing file; without it an existing file is refused), `--size WxH` (even, default 1920x1080), `--fps N` (default 60), `--rate-control cbr|vbr|cq` (default cbr), `--bitrate KBPS` (the CBR rate or VBR target, default 12000; refused with cq), `--max-bitrate KBPS` (VBR peak, at least the target, default 1.5 x the target; vbr only), `--quality N` (cq level 0 best to 51, default 23; cq only), `--keyframe-interval S` (whole seconds, default 2), `--b-frames N` (at most, 0 to 4, default 0), `--profile NAME` (codec profile, default the encoder's choice), `--preset quality|balanced|speed` (default balanced), `--cbr-padding` (with cbr: the `cbr_padded` mode, filler data to the full bitrate), `--audio-bitrate KBPS` (default 160), `--encoder ID` (a video encoder adapter id or its last dotted part: only that encoder is used, no fallback; an unknown id is an error), `--display N`, `--camera N` (indices from `devices`), `--no-camera` (the default recording includes the first camera; this flag leaves it out), `--no-mic`, `--no-system-audio`, `--no-audio`, `--mic-offset MS` and `--system-audio-offset MS` (sync offsets in whole ms, within ±200: positive delays that source, negative plays it earlier; the input clamps an advance to the audio graph's latency minus a period minus 5 ms, 35 ms by default). Bad arguments exit with code 2 and the usage text. The video settings are printed in one line before the start. While recording, one line per second shows frames exported and encoded, drops and skips, each source and the packets muxed; the summary at the end splits video frames into exported, encoded, dropped at export, skipped before the encoder and dropped at the encoder.
- The scene: one canvas of the given size and rate, one scene on air with the chosen display filling the canvas (cursor on, at the canvas rate), the camera at 1280x720 30 fps in a 16:9 rectangle 4/15 of the canvas wide, a margin of 1/40 of the width from the bottom-right corner, the default microphone and the default system audio. A type with no target is left out and said so; no display is an error. Before the scene is built the camera's and microphone's adapters are asked for capture permission (`request_permission`, which may show the OS prompt and waits for the answer); a refused or unanswered one is left out and said so, like an absent one, so a missing permission never fails the start (an audio source opens at instantiation, which is strict) and never adds a source that cannot run.
- Adapters: Matroska and fragmented MP4 take H.264, length-prefixed HEVC (an hvcC record) and AV1 (OBU samples, an av1C record; `V_AV1` in Matroska, an `av01` sample entry in fragmented MP4) plus AAC; the file transport is on every platform. The desktop recording asks for H.264 unless its profile says HEVC or AV1, and only when an encoder lists that codec. A profile whose format is WAV asks for PCM and no video encoder, so a computer with no video encoder can still record that one file of the mix. The PCM width is 16, 24 or 32 (float), passed as the `bit_depth` adapter option; absent is 16. Settings Format is Video or Audio, then the container: Video is MKV, MP4 or MOV (fragmented QuickTime, with the same video codecs and AAC), Audio is WAV or FLAC. FLAC is 16-bit or 24-bit integer. It asks for the FLAC encoder and no video encoder. `bit_depth` is 16 or 24; 32 is refused, and choosing FLAC while 32-bit float is stored saves 16. Video settings stay saved for the next MKV or MP4 recording. A stream stays H.264: RTMP does not carry HEVC or AV1, and MPEG-TS (SRT) does not carry them yet. Windows adds WGC and Media Foundation capture, WASAPI, NVENC and AMF (in that order), Media Foundation AAC; NVENC offers AV1 Main when the GPU's probe lists it (an RTX 40-series board does). macOS adds ScreenCaptureKit and AVFoundation capture, Core Audio, VideoToolbox (hardware, then Apple's software encoders; H.264 and HEVC) and AudioToolbox AAC; Linux adds PipeWire capture (cameras, and displays and windows through the screen-cast portal's picker: `record` shows the portal dialog when no display is listed), PipeWire audio, NVENC, AMF, QSV and VA-API (in that order) and FFmpeg's AAC encoder (`rbs-codec-ffmpeg`, the system's libavcodec loaded at run time). A platform without video or audio encoders says recording is not available there yet.
- The engine gets `OsThreadPolicy` (ADR-0030). The device is `GpuRequest::default()` (high performance, no software adapter). The file path is made absolute and turned into a `file://` URL from its components: a drive path is `file:///C:/...`, a UNC path `file://server/share/...` (verbatim `\\?\` prefixes included), and `%`, space, `#`, `?` and a `\` inside a name are escaped. When the start fails after the file was created, `rbs-cli` removes it.
- Exit code 0 when the recording ran and stopped cleanly with packets muxed (`Progress::packets_muxed`); 1 when start failed, the recording failed while running, the engine did not stop cleanly, or no packet was muxed. Ctrl+C (and, on Windows, Ctrl+Break or closing the console) sets the same stop as Enter: the loop calls `end` and `finish`, so the trailer is written. The handler only stores a flag, noticed on the next service step (at most 50 ms). A second Ctrl+C while that stop is already asked lets the process end. When the handler cannot be installed, Enter still stops.
- `rbs-cli stream <rtmp[s]://host[:port]/app | srt://host:port> [options]`: the same scene and options as `record` except `--overwrite`, with `--bitrate` defaulting to 6000 kbit/s. `rtmp://` and `rtmps://` send H.264 and AAC packets to the RTMP transport (no muxer; `rtmps` over TLS, ADR-0127); `srt://` muxes MPEG-TS for the SRT transport; any other scheme is refused when the arguments are parsed. The stream key (RTMP, required) or stream id (SRT, optional) is read from the environment variable `RBS_STREAM_KEY`, or the one `--key-env NAME` names, never from the command line, and is trimmed; a missing RTMP key fails before any device is opened or permission asked; it goes into `RecordSettings::secret` only, so it is in no URL, log line or report. `--srt-latency MS` (0 to 65535, SRT only) becomes the `latency_ms` transport option. Reconnect is on by default (`Backoff`, 1 s doubling to 30 s, 10 attempts; with the transports' rule that attempts restart after a connection that reached publish), `--no-reconnect` turns it off; a server that rejects the key is not retried and stops the stream with the error. Lines and summary as for `record`, with the URL instead of the file; each connection change is printed once when it happens (`connected to <url>`, `connected to <url> again (n times)`, `not connected, trying again (attempt n)`), the per-second line ends with `live` or `not connected`, and the summary adds connections, reconnect attempts and transport drops. Exit code 1 also when no packet reached the stream, or when the stream never connected (`never connected to <url>`, followed by the cause when the output failed, such as the connection error with `--no-reconnect`).
- Stream adapters on every platform: the MPEG-TS muxer, the RTMP and SRT transports (pure Rust, no FFmpeg).
- `rbs-cli vcam status|add|format|remove` (ADR-0066, ADR-0070; Windows 11, elsewhere it exits with code 1 and "not available on this platform"): `status` prints the camera's state in words (`added`, `not added`, `update needed: <reason>`, `not available on this computer: <reason>`); `add [--size WxH] [--fps N]` adds the camera (`install`: one administrator prompt when the source class is not registered yet) and then sets its format; `format [--size WxH] [--fps N]` changes the format (`set_format`) and says when it applies only once no app uses the camera; `remove` removes the camera (`uninstall`; the class registration stays). Sizes are 1920x1080, 1280x720 (default) or 640x360, rates 15, 24, 30 (default) or 60 (ADR-0066 decision 11); anything else exits with code 2. The adapter keeps the camera's symbolic link in `vcam-link.txt` in the RBS settings folder (the `LinkStore`). `rbs-vcam-setup.exe` and `rbs_vcam_mf_source.dll` must be next to `rbs-cli.exe` (build `-p rbs-cli -p rbs-vcam-mf -p rbs-vcam-mf-source`); outside Program Files, `add` needs a development build with `--features rbs-vcam-mf/dev-any-location`.
- `record` and `stream` take `--vcam` (with `--vcam-size WxH` and `--vcam-fps N`, same values and defaults as `vcam format`): after the start, the camera's format is set and `Recording::start_virtual_camera` sends the program to the camera for the whole run; a camera that is not added fails the command before it records (`--vcam: not installed`). The per-second line adds `camera <n> delivered, <n> with no app reading, <n> waiting for a format change; <n> handed over, <n> skipped (app behind), <n> skipped (readback busy), <n> lost, <n> refused, <n> failed` (the first part is the session's counters, `VirtualCameraState::session`, when the adapter shares them; the rest is the node's), and the summary repeats it with the node's failure, if any. This is how the P15 measurements (camera off and on next to a recording) and the hardware test run without the desktop app.
- `rbs-cli devices` lists installed source types with numbered targets, and every registered adapter as available or unavailable.

## Separate files (ADR-0086)

A source may be marked to record its picture, its sound, or both as files of their own (`Source.record_separately`, scene model). The binder does not read the flag, so a source that is not in the program mix is not kept running for a recording (decision 8 is a later step). `iso::plan(collection)` names the files and opens nothing:

- Sources in id order. A flag that is both false is skipped.
- A built-in audio source with `video` is `IsoError::VideoOnAudioOnly`. A built-in picture source that has no sound of its own (capture, still, browser) with `audio` is `AudioOnPictureOnly`. A media file, and a type this build does not classify, may set either switch.
- A `video` flag's form follows `video_as` (ADR-0117 decisions 1 and 2): `Auto` is an image track (`IsoRole::Image`) for a still source (`SourceClass::Still`: image and colour; asked of the class, never of a type id) and a video track otherwise; `Video` is always a video track; `Image` is an image track and, on a source that is not still, `IsoError::ImageOnMovingSource`, except a text source, which then runs as `Auto` (ADR-0129 decision 1, reported as `SourceSettingsAdjusted`). Every built-in filter keeps a still source still. A source marked for both feeds follows the same rule for its picture.
- At most `MAX_ISO_VIDEO` (16) video tracks, `MAX_ISO_AUDIO` (32) audio tracks and `MAX_ISO_IMAGES` (32) image tracks; image tracks are not counted in `MAX_ISO_VIDEO`. More is `TooManyVideo`, `TooManyAudio` or `TooManyImages`. `IsoError` is `#[non_exhaustive]`.
- Each file name is a single name: letters, numbers, space, `-`, `_` and `.` from the source name, other characters become `_`, a trailing dot or space is dropped, and a blank or reserved device name (`CON`, `PRN`, `AUX`, `NUL`, `COM1`–`COM9`, `LPT1`–`LPT9`) becomes `source-<id>`. A source marked for both feeds uses `<name>-video` and `<name>-audio`. A name already used gets ` 2`, ` 3`, and so on. Names are compared case-folded, as the Windows and macOS file systems do (`Mic` and `mic` give `Mic` and `mic 2`), and `master` (`MASTER_STEM`) is reserved for the master file, so a source named `Master` gives `Master 2`.

`RecordingManifest::at_start(plan, master_file, t0_utc, t0_ns)` builds the manifest written at start. `master_file` is one name, not a path (`MasterName` otherwise); `t0_utc` is the caller's wall-clock text and must not be empty (`Clock`). `to_toml` writes `format = 1`, `kind = "recording-manifest"`, `t0_utc`, `t0_ns`, then one `[[files]]` table per file (`role` of `master`, `video`, `audio` or `image`, `name`, and for a track `source` and `source_name`). It has no directory, no secret, and no `start_offset_ns`: offsets and drop counters are written when the recording stops.

`Studio::start_iso_audio` opens the audio files of that plan and no others:

- One audio-only file per audio flag, in the request's container, using the request's audio codec. AAC in the master's container is the default. PCM (16-bit, or 24-bit or 32-bit float when `bit_depth` is set) in `Wav` is available (ADR-0085). FLAC in `Flac` is available (16-bit, or 24-bit when `bit_depth` is set). When the codec is PCM or FLAC and `bit_depth` is set, that width is the `bit_depth` adapter option, the same rule as the main recording (`"16"`, `"24"` or, for PCM only, `"32"`). Absent is 16-bit. Other codecs are not given the option. The tap is the source pin the mixer hears: after the source's filters, before the fader and mute, so a muted strip still records.
- Each file is `{folder_url}/{name}.{ext}` (`mkv`, `mp4`, `ts`, `flv`, `wav` or `flac`). A space, `%`, `#` or `?` in the name is percent-encoded in the URL. The manifest's audio `name` includes the extension; a video `name` stays the plan's stem.
- The files share `ISO_AUDIO_ENCODER` (partition 17, one worker for every audio file) and `ISO_OUTPUT` (partition 18, one worker for every file). They are not studio outputs, so `MAX_OUTPUTS` is unchanged. The partitions are added on the first audio start. A worker exists only while a file is open.
- No audio flag: the call returns the manifest and adds no node, partition or worker.
- A marked audio source the program mix is not hearing is `IsoError::NotHeard`, and nothing is opened. With `skip_unheard` set, such a source is left out instead: it is not in the manifest and is listed in `IsoAudioStarted::not_heard`; when it was the only one, nothing is opened. A video flag is listed and not opened.
- One failure aborts the start and removes anything it opened. A second start while files are open is `RecordError::IsoRunning`. `manifest_dir`, when set, receives `recording.rbs.toml` after the files are open; if that write fails, the files are closed and the error is `IsoError::Manifest` (no path in the message). The master file named in the manifest is not opened by this call.

`Studio::start_iso_video` opens the video files of that plan (ADR-0086 step 3.2; not yet used by the desktop app):

- One video-only file per video flag, `{folder_url}/{name}.{ext}`, in the request's container, with the request's `VideoSettings` (decision 4: the caller passes the master's or the profile's). Each file has its own `VideoExportNode` and `VideoEncoderNode`.
- The picture is the source's own, without its filters, by default (ADR-0119 decision 1): the export reads the source node (`SceneBinder::source_node`) through a normalisation pass (`rbs_filters::Normalise`, an identity filter that decodes the source's picture into the canvas format). With `IsoVideoStart::include_filters` the picture is taken after the filters instead (ADR-0086 decision 3): the export reads the source's chain tail (`SceneBinder::chain_tail`), its last enabled filter, whose output is already in the canvas format, and a source with no filter goes through the normalisation pass. The export and that pass run on `RENDER`, after the master's.
- The file's size is the source's picture size at the start (`picture_size`), rounded up to even. A marked source with no picture yet (not drawn, or capture not open) is `IsoError::NoPicture`, and nothing is opened. Sources draw only while something takes the canvas, so the master starts first. A source whose size changes during the take loses its frames (`lost`); `size: master`, `max_height`, a per-source filter choice, off-air sources and offsets from `t0` are later steps.
- Each export converts on every render tick and stamps the frame with the tick, so the file runs at the canvas frame rate even when the source's picture did not change (a still, a paused source, a camera slower than the canvas).
- Each export gives way (decision 9): when the render tick before ran longer than `give_way_share` of the frame period (`DEFAULT_GIVE_WAY_SHARE`, 0.75), it skips that canvas and counts `gave_way`, so a late tick does not grow later for the master. It also gives way for one second whenever a frame was dropped on the way into a running master's video encoder (`yield_to` with the sum of those queues' `video_drops`): hardware encoders share one media engine, so without this the master lost about 20 % of its frames with four 1080p60 files on an M4 Pro (3.2 measurement). A drop again within a second after a hold ends doubles the next hold, up to eight seconds; with five 1080p60 H.264 sessions on an M4 Pro the files then give way most of the time, so the ADR-0087 limits (step 3.5) must keep a take inside what the machine's encoders sustain. `IsoVideoTrack::encoder` gives each file's encoder counters (`submitted` is the frames in the file) and `IsoVideoTrack::queue` the queue into its encoder (`video_drops`: frames the encoder never took because its partition was busy with the other files on it).
- The encoders take turns on the first two of `ISO_VIDEO_ENCODERS` (partitions 19 and 20, frame cadence a quarter period after the render tick, as the master's encoders) while they use the master's engine; partitions 21 to 34 are for encoders that do not share it (ADR-0119 steps (c) and (d)). A partition per file on the master's engine was measured and made the master miss frames (ADR-0119 `## Review`, step (a)). The files share `ISO_OUTPUT` with the audio files. They are not studio outputs. A partition has a worker only while a file on it is open.
- **Image tracks (ADR-0117).** The same call opens the image tracks of the plan. Each needs `IsoVideoStart::image_dir`, the take folder (`IsoError::ImageFolder` without it); `t0` times the images. The picture is the source node's own (straight sRGB `Rgba8`, copied with no pass), or its chain tail with `include_filters` (converted once per change). An `ImageTrackNode` on `RENDER` and a `PngWriterNode` on `ISO_OUTPUT` per track: no encoder, no encoder partition, no transport. The writer encodes each picture with the `png` crate (8-bit RGBA, straight alpha, `sRGB` chunk) and writes `<stem>.png`, then `<stem>-2.png`, ... into `image_dir` directly, as the manifest is written (a temporary `.<name>.part`, then a rename; the owner's choice of 2026-11-14 over a transport, recorded in ADR-0117 `## Review`). `IsoVideoStarted::images` lists each track: `file_name` (the first image), `size`, `stats` (`ImageTrackStats`) and `files` (`ImageFiles::entries()`, the `ImageEntry` list for the manifest, and `failed`).
- **Following the source.** When an update replaces the node a track reads (a still source rebuilt for a new colour or file, a filter added or removed), `Studio::update` relinks each running track to the source's current producer before its apply: an image track to any producer, a video track only to a producer of the same kind (a source node through its normalisation pass, or a filter tail). The new node's first frame has a new generation, so an image track writes it as a new image.
- **Limits and skips.** `IsoVideoStart::max_video` (default `MAX_ISO_VIDEO`) caps the video files: the marked sources past it, in source id order, are left out (`IsoSkip::Limit`); image tracks are not counted. With `skip_no_picture`, a marked source with no picture is left out (`IsoSkip::NoPicture`) instead of refusing the start. Both are listed in `IsoVideoStarted::skipped` (source, role, reason), in source id order; when everything is left out nothing is added.
- No video flag: nothing is added. One failure removes anything the call added. A second start while video files are open is `RecordError::IsoRunning`. `stop_iso_video` drains and drops every node it added.

### In the desktop app (protocol 1.46, picture files 1.52)

The studio host (`rbs-app`) opens the separate audio files with the main canvas's recording. It does so only when the request asks for them (`RecordRequest::iso`: true for the desktop's Record, "Record when the stream starts" and `record.start` with `iso` absent or true; false for `record.start_to_file` and `iso: false`) and a source of the collection is marked for its sound or its picture. `Command::SetRecordSeparately` takes a `RecordPatch` (`enabled`, `video`, `audio`, `video_as`, each optional) with the rules of `source.set_record_separately` (control-api, protocol 1.52): `enabled` is a picture source's picture and anything else's sound. Then:

- The recording goes into a folder named like the file: `<folder>/<name>.<ext>` becomes `<folder>/<name>/master.<ext>` (`MASTER_STEM` and the container's extension). The host makes the leaf folder with `create_dir`, which fails when it exists, so nothing outside a folder this start made is written or removed (decision 7).
- `t0` is the engine clock at the start and the UTC wall clock, with milliseconds. The audio files open first: 24-bit PCM in `Wav` through `start_iso_audio` with `skip_unheard`, so a marked source the program mix does not hear is listed in `skipped` and the take starts. The profile section of decision 4 (`recording.iso`) and its settings come with the Separate files card; until then this is the only format.
- `recording.rbs.toml` is then written into the folder with `iso::write_manifest`, listing the master and the audio files.
- **Picture files (1.52).** Once the master runs, `start_separate_pictures` opens the video files and image tracks with `start_iso_video`: the folder as `folder_url` and `image_dir`, the recording's container and video settings, `t0` from the manifest, `max_video` from the estimate (`Studio::separate_video_plan`, `estimate` with this computer's calibration and the cached free space of the profile's recording folder, `separate_video_limit`; [output-estimate.md](output-estimate.md#separate-video-limit): the declared session limit, else `SEPARATE_VIDEO_LIMIT`, 3) and `skip_no_picture`. What it skips is listed in `skipped` (`limit`, `no_picture`); a WAV or FLAC take skips every picture source (`audio_only`), and a start that fails skips them all (`open_failed`): the take goes on. The opened files are added to `tracks` and the manifest, which is written again. At stop the picture files stop after the master, before the audio files, and each image track's pictures and counters (`[[files.images]]`, `coalesced`, `over_cap`) go into the manifest's stop write.
- The master starts last. When it cannot, the audio files are stopped and every name the plan could have written, the master, the manifest and the folder (when then empty) are removed. When the audio files cannot open, the same names are removed and the master does not start.
- The host tracks the files together with the master, after the start's last panic point. Recovery after a panic (ADR-0055) stops audio files when no main recording is tracked. A take that panicked may leave its files in its folder; they are not removed.
- Stopping the main recording stops the audio files too, after the master's tail. Deleting a marked source during the take leaves its file valid and the take running.
- Offsets from `t0` are not written yet: the WAVs start before the master by the master's start-up time.
- `iso: false`, the vertical canvas and no flag at all record one file, as before 1.46.
- UI (ADR-0086 step 2):
  - Properties › Audio of a sound source has a *Recording* section with a *Record separately* Toggle (`RecordPatch::enabled`). A picture source has its *Recording* section below the tabs, with the other source-wide sections (step 3.5): the Toggle marks the picture (a media file: both feeds), and once on a still source shows *Recorded as* *Automatic (image)* / *Image* / *Video* and a media file *Picture and sound* / *Picture* / *Sound*. While a take runs each says the mark applies to the next recording.
  - Settings › Recording › *Separate files*: *Record separate files* (the output profile's `recording.iso` section, `SeparateFiles { format_version, enabled, on_hardware_limit }`, on by default; off, Record and `record.start` without `iso` write one file; `on_hardware_limit` is `ask` or `skip`, `ask` by default, the policy of a control API 1.54 start that does not name one, kept as read so a later build's value is saved back unchanged and behaves as `ask`; it has no control in the card yet, step F), *Sources* (what the marks write, "2 video · 1 image · 1 sound", with the names behind (i)), *Video files* (same encoder as the recording), *Sound files* (WAV, 24-bit) and *Limit* (`SEPARATE_VIDEO_LIMIT`, a warning when more are marked).
  - While recording, the Outputs panel's recording card has a *Separate files* line naming each file's source and kind, "Camera (video), Logo (images), Mic (sound)" (`RecordingUpdate::Started::separate`).

## Recording manifest (ADR-0086 decision 6, ADR-0089)

`rbs-manifest` holds the manifest model, its TOML text and its reader; `rbs-record` re-exports `RecordingManifest`, `ManifestFile` and `SourceRef` and builds the start manifest (`iso::manifest_at_start`). The crate has no engine, port, adapter or UI dependency (`rbs-core` and `toml` only).

- **Where.** A recording folder (ADR-0086) has `recording.rbs.toml` (`FOLDER_MANIFEST`). A plain recording that has markers, or the editor project setting on, writes `<file stem>.rbs.toml` next to its file (`manifest_name_for`; ADR-0089 decision 5 narrows ADR-0086 decision 6 here, owner's choice of 2026-11-14). `write(dir, name, manifest)` writes a temporary `.<name>.tmp`, then renames it; the error message has no path.
- **One owner.** The recording's state on the control thread holds the manifest in memory; the start write, marker flushes (a one-shot 5-second timer armed by the first marker not yet on disk) and the stop rewrite each rewrite the whole file through it. No marker, no timer.
- **Text.** `format = 1`, `kind = "recording-manifest"`, `t0_utc`, `t0_ns`. The stop rewrite adds `complete = true`, `duration_ns` (from `t0`) and, for a recording with a picture, `width`, `height` and `frame_duration` (`"num/den"` seconds per frame, for example `"1001/60000"`). Then one `[[files]]` table per file: `role` (`master`, `video`, `audio`, `image`), `name` (a single file name), for a separate track `source` (the scene source id) and `source_name`; the stop rewrite adds `start_offset_ns` (the file's first sample from `t0`), `duration_ns` (its end is `start_offset_ns + duration_ns`), for a picture `width`, `height`, `frame_duration`, for sound `sample_rate` and `channels`; an image track (ADR-0117 decision 6) has its counters `coalesced` and `over_cap` at stop and one `[[files.images]]` table per picture, in the order taken: `name`, `width`, `height` and `at_ns` (the render tick's time from `t0`), written after the file's own keys. Then one `[[skipped]]` table per marked source the take did not write (control API 1.54): `source`, `source_name`, `role` (`video`, `image`, `audio`) and `reason` (the `record.status` value: `limit`, `no_picture`, `open_failed`, ...); a skipped track is never in `[[files]]`. The start manifest has the skips decided before the files open (the start gate's and the sound files'), the write after the picture files open adds theirs, and the stop rewrite keeps them. A one-file take has no manifest for them. Then one `[[markers]]` table per marker: `index` (1-based, in the order added), `t_ns` (from `t0`, after the lead), `pressed_ns` (manual markers), `kind` (`manual`, `scene`, `protection`, `stream_start`, `stream_end`), `label`, `color` (`blue`, `red`, `orange`, `yellow`, `green`, `purple`). No directory, no secret.
- **Reading.** `RecordingManifest::from_toml` refuses another `kind` (`ReadError::Kind`), a `format` above 1 (`Format`), and a missing or mistyped field or a `name` that is not one file name (`Field(key)`); unknown keys are ignored, and so are tables a format 1 reader does not know (a later minor may add some, as 1.54 added `[[skipped]]`). `is_complete()` is true only after the stop rewrite.
- **Markers.** `MarkerLog::new(t0_ns, lead)` (lead at most 30 s, `MarkerError::LeadTooLong`) keeps one recording's markers. `add_manual(now_ns, label?, color?)`: `t_ns = now - t0 - lead`, never below 0, `pressed_ns = now - t0`; a blank label is "Marker N", N counting manual markers. `add_auto(now_ns, kind, label, color)` ignores the lead. A label is trimmed, one line (`LabelControl`) of at most 200 Unicode scalar values (`LabelTooLong`). At most 10 000 markers (`Full`). A refused marker changes nothing. `has_pending` and `mark_flushed` drive the flush timer (below).
- **In the desktop app (protocol 1.50).** Each running recording of the studio host has a marker log from the moment its output started (a recording folder uses the separate files' `t0`). `marker.add` and `marker.remove` change it on the control thread. When the recording stops, after its output is closed, the host writes the manifest with the markers: the start manifest of a recording folder rewritten with them, or for a plain recording `<file stem>.rbs.toml` listing the master and the markers, only when it has a marker. A write failure is added to the stopped recording's failure text and does not touch the media. `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/record.rs` `markers_go_into_the_manifest_when_the_recording_stops` covers both, the refusals and the rate limit's session budget.
- **The stop rewrite (ADR-0089 decision 6, protocol 1.58).** A file's timing comes from the output node that wrote it (I/O nodes spec, output node invariant 8): `first_pts_ns` is its first sample (the muxer's media time 0) and `end_ns` its end. Only outputs that need it keep it, chosen when they are built (P13, owner 2026-11-15): every separate file, and the master of a take with separate files or with the editor project setting (`OutputSettings::track_span`). Any other recording runs no span code and its manifest stays incomplete. `Studio::stop_output`'s `FinishReport::media` and `Studio::take_iso_media` (the separate files closed since the last call, by name) give a `MediaSpan` (`first_ns`, `end_ns`, picture and sound from the output's stream descriptors); `MediaSpan::timing(t0)` gives the stop fields. At stop the host sets each file's timing (an image track has none; a file that wrote nothing keeps none), moves `t0` earlier to the earliest first sample when one came before it (`RecordingManifest::move_origin`: markers, offsets and image times shift with it, so nothing moves on the master clock), and, only when the master has timing, sets `complete = true` with the latest file end as `duration_ns` and the master's picture (`RecordingManifest::complete`). A separate file's end includes the audio that still flows until the drain (Open questions). The project writer then cuts connected clips at the master's end.
- **Pauses (ADR-0124 decision 10).** `RecordingManifest::pauses` (`[[pauses]]`: `at_file_ns`, the join in file time; `from_ns` and `to_ns`, master time from `t0`; `join_error_ns` for a cut at a shared output) and `paused_ns()` (written as top-level `paused_ns` when there is a pause). Every other time is file time with the pauses taken out, so a reader that knows nothing of pauses still places everything right. `PauseSpans` maps master time to file time (`to_file`, `last_join_before`, `rows(end)`, an open pause ending at the end). A marker log follows its recording's pauses (`MarkerLog::pause(at_ns)`, `resume(at_ns)`; a resume at or before the pause's beginning cancels it): a marker's `t_ns` and `pressed_ns` are file times; one pressed while paused sits on that pause's join with `while_paused = true` (the lead does not apply); a lead never reaches back past the join of the last pause before the press. `while_paused` is written only when true. In the desktop app (protocol 1.63) each pause and resume of a recording (`record.pause`, `record.resume`, `record.pause_toggle`) feeds its marker log at the pause's instant, and the manifest written at stop lists the pauses, a pause still open at stop ending at the stop; a pause alone does not make a plain recording write its manifest. `join_error_ns` is not written yet. `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/stream.rs` `markers_while_paused_sit_on_the_join_and_the_manifest_lists_the_pause` covers it.
- **Marker settings (ADR-0089 decisions 3 and 4, protocol 1.62).** The output profile's `recording` section has a `[markers]` table (`MarkerSettings`): `lead_ms` (0 to 30 000, default 0; more is refused by `settings.set` and by the profile check), `scene_changes` (default on), `stream_events` (default off) and `pause_markers` (default on, protocol 1.63: the editor project written at stop has a marker per pause, ADR-0124 decision 10). A table with only defaults is still read as written; missing keys take their defaults. The settings are copied into the recording when it starts (`RecordRequest::markers`), so a change applies to the next recording. `lead_ms` is the lead of the recording's `MarkerLog`: manual markers only.
- **Automatic markers in the desktop app.** *Scene changes* (`scene`, purple, labelled with the new program scene's name): after each command the host applies, every running recording whose canvas's program scene differs from the one it last saw gets a marker at that moment, on the engine clock (the tick the command is applied, which is when the transition starts). The main canvas's recording follows the main program; the vertical canvas's recording follows its own program only when it does not follow the main canvas, else the main program (decision 3). Nested scenes are never marked. *Stream start and end* (`stream_start` "Stream started" green, `stream_end` "Stream ended" red): when a stream of that canvas starts or ends while the recording runs. Each kind only when its setting is on; an automatic marker makes a plain recording write its manifest like a manual one. Off, the host does no work for them beyond comparing one scene id per command per running recording. `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/record.rs` `scene_markers_and_the_marker_lead_follow_the_request` and `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/stream.rs` `stream_markers_mark_a_running_recording_when_the_profile_asks` cover them.
- **In the window (ADR-0089 decision 10).** `studio_host::Command::AddMarker` runs `marker.add` for every running recording and `Command::RemoveMarkers` runs `marker.remove` for each (canvas, index); every add or remove, from the window or a control API client, sends `Update::Markers { counts, placed }`: each canvas's manual marker count (`None` while it does not record) and, after an add, a `MarkerNote` (label, `t_ns`, and what Undo removes). The controls are in the [design system](ui-design-system.md) (Outputs, Recording settings). `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/record.rs` `the_window_adds_and_takes_back_a_marker` covers it.
- **Marker flush (ADR-0089 decision 5).** While a recording runs, a marker not yet on disk (an add, an automatic marker or a remove) arms that recording's one-shot timer for 5 seconds on the host clock (`MARKER_FLUSH_NS`); when it is due the host rewrites the manifest with the markers so far and the pauses up to now, not complete, through the same temporary name and rename. The timer is not re-armed until the markers change again; the stop rewrite still runs and replaces it. A plain recording whose markers were all taken back loses the manifest a flush wrote (at the next flush or at stop). A write failure is an `Update::Error` and the recording goes on. No marker, no timer: the host only reads one flag per running recording each round. `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/record.rs` `markers_reach_the_manifest_while_recording` covers it.
- **Chapters in Matroska (ADR-0089 decision 14).** After a clean stop (no failure) whose stop rewrite succeeded, a Matroska master with markers and a known timing (its span is tracked: separate files or the editor project setting; a plain take without them gets no chapters by default, owner 2026-11-16) gets them as chapters through the composition root's `ChapterWriter` (`HostConfig::chapters`, ADR-0159; `rbs-app` passes `MatroskaChapters`, which calls `rbs_mux_matroska::append_chapters`; without one the markers stay in the manifest only): one per marker, sorted by time, at `t_ns - start_offset_ns` from the file's first sample (a marker before it is on it), titled with its label; a marker after the master's end is left out (the manifest keeps it). A file that cannot take chapters (another adapter's, an unclean end) is left as it is without an error; a read or write failure is added to the recording's failure ("The markers could not be added to the file as chapters: ..."). The manifest stays the source of truth. The muxer's header carries a 128-byte `Void` for the `SeekHead` (crate docs). Checked by `rbs-mux-matroska` `tests/output.rs` (`chapters_*`), `tests/ffmpeg_interop.rs` (ffprobe reads the chapters at the right times; ignored without ffmpeg) and `crates/rbs-studio-host/tests/studio/record.rs` `slow_a_matroska_master_gets_its_markers_as_chapters`. Players were not checked by hand yet (mpv, VLC).
- **Not built yet (ADR-0089 order of work).** Protection markers (ADR-0090); chapters in MP4 and MOV (written fragmented, a chapter track needs a rewritten `moov`) and in separate files.

## Editor project (ADR-0089)

`rbs-project` (depends on `rbs-core` and `rbs-manifest`; used only by composition roots) turns a complete manifest into an editor project. It never opens a media file.

- `build(&manifest, &MediaFolder, format, title)` (`Format::Fcpxml` or `Format::Xmeml`) -> Result<String, ProjectError>` is pure. `MediaFolder::from_path` (absolute only, `NotAbsolute`) or `from_text` turns a folder into a `file://` URL: `C:\x` and `C:/x` give `file:///C:/x/` (the drive colon is literal), `\\host\share\x` gives `file://host/share/x/` (the host is not encoded), `/Users/x` gives `file:///Users/x/`; every other byte outside the RFC 3986 unreserved set is percent-encoded as UTF-8.
- Errors: `Incomplete` (no `complete = true`), `NoMaster`, `NoPicture` (a sound-only master; no project format yet), `MissingTiming(name)` (a file without its stop fields, a `video` file without a picture or an `audio` file without sound), `UnknownRole(role)`, `UnsupportedRate(num/den)` (xmeml only: a rate that is neither whole nor NTSC).
- **FCPXML 1.9**, one `.fcpxml` file. Resources: one `format` per distinct picture size and rate (the sequence's first), then one `asset` per file (`start="0s"`, its duration in its own frames or samples, `hasVideo`/`format`, `hasAudio`/`audioSources`/`audioChannels`/`audioRate`) with a `media-rep kind="original-media"` URL. One event and project named `title`; the sequence uses the canvas format, `tcStart="0s"`, `tcFormat="NDF"`, `audioLayout` from the master's channels (`mono`, `stereo`, `surround`) and `audioRate` from its sample rate.
- **Timeline.** Sequence time 0 is `t0`. The master's offset and length are rounded to the nearest frame (half up); an offset of one frame or more puts a `gap` before it on the spine. A separate picture file is a connected clip on lane 1, 2, ... at its nearest frame. A separate sound file is a connected clip on lane -1, -2, ... at the first frame at or after its first sample, with the remainder as its in-point (`start`, in samples), and ends at the last whole frame of its sound, so every sample stays where it was recorded. Clips and markers that begin before the master's first frame hang off the gap, all others off the master clip; `offset` and `start` are in that parent's time. A clip past the storyline's end is shortened to it; one starting after it is left out. Markers are sorted by time, rounded down to a frame and kept on the storyline (a marker after the end is on its last frame); `scene` markers are `chapter-marker`s, the others `marker`s with the label as `value`; colours are dropped.
- **FCP7 XML (`xmeml` version 5)**, one `.xml` file, for DaVinci Resolve (which drops FCPXML's markers, below) and Premiere Pro. One sequence named `title` at the canvas rate: `timebase` and `ntsc` (`FALSE` for a whole rate, `TRUE` with the rounded-up base for `N*1000/1001`), timecode `00:00:00:00` `NDF`. Video track 1 is the master's picture at the frame nearest its offset (rounded as above), from frame 0 of the file (`in` 0); each separate picture file is its own video track (2, 3, ...). Every file with sound has one audio track per channel (`sourcetrack` 1, 2, ...; Resolve makes a mono track of each): the master's first, linked (`link`, `groupindex` 1) to its picture so they move together, then each separate sound file's, linked to each other. Every clip is at the frame nearest its first sample with `in` 0: xmeml positions are whole frames, so a separate sound file sits up to half a frame from its recorded place (FCPXML keeps the sample). The same shortening and leaving out past the end as FCPXML. A `file` (`name`, `pathurl` as `file://localhost/...` for a local path, its rate, duration in sequence frames, a timecode `00:00:00:00`, picture size, and `samplerate` and `channelcount` with `depth` 16) is written in full once and then referenced by `id`; without the timecode Resolve 21 does not find the file on disk. Markers are sorted and rounded as above and are sequence markers (`name` is the label, `out` -1): Resolve 21 drops the clip markers of a clip it imports from disk. Kinds and colours are dropped (the format has none).
- **When it runs (ADR-0089 decision 10, protocol 1.58; `xmeml` protocol 1.60).** The recording profile's `editor_project` (`"fcpxml"` or `"xmeml"`; absent is off) writes the project when the recording stops, after the stop rewrite, through `export`; a failure is added to the stopped recording's failure text and the media and manifest stay. `project.export {recording, format}` (control API) and `rbs-cli project --manifest <path> [--format fcpxml|xmeml] [--strict]` call the same `export` on demand. A manifest without `complete = true` is refused (`Incomplete`): the app says the take was recorded without separate files or the setting, or did not stop cleanly. No UI yet (the Settings row is a visual step).
- **Pause markers (ADR-0124 decision 10).** The project written at stop follows the recording's `pause_markers` setting; `project.export` and `rbs-cli project` use the default (on). `build_with` and `export_with` take `Options` (`pause_markers`, on by default; `build` and `export` use the default): one marker per `[[pauses]]` row on its join, labelled "Paused" and the time left out (`2:10`, or `1:02:10` from an hour, whole seconds), a plain marker (not a chapter), written before the markers on the same frame. With `pause_markers` off only the manifest's markers are written.
- `export(folder, manifest_name, format, strict)` reads the manifest (at most 8 MiB), refuses an incomplete one, leaves out separate files that are not in the folder (listed in `Exported::missing`; with `strict`, `MissingFile`), refuses a missing master (`MissingMaster`), and writes `<stem>.fcpxml` or `<stem>.xml` next to the manifest (the folder's name for `recording.rbs.toml`), or `<stem> 2.fcpxml`, ... with `create_new`, so nothing is replaced.
- **Supported editors.** None yet. DaVinci Resolve is the default target (owner, 2026-11-14): it is imported and checked first, then Final Cut Pro. **DaVinci Resolve 21.1.1 (Windows, 2026-11-15)** imports FCPXML 1.8, 1.9 and 1.10 projects from real takes (mp4, mov, mkv) with their clips, picture and sound, but drops every marker (nine variants tried: clip markers, chapter markers, gap and no gap, 1.8 and 1.10, a `clip` with `video` instead of `asset-clip`), and writes none into its own FCPXML export; an FCP7 XML imports them by name at the right frames (all blue) as sequence markers, when each `file` has a timecode (without it the file is not found) and with one mono audio track per channel. So xmeml is the Resolve format (owner, 2026-11-15) and FCPXML the Final Cut format. The format is announced only after a manual import in DaVinci Resolve and Final Cut Pro (clips placed and aligned, the sound clips' offsets and in-points kept, markers and chapter markers visible). Record the editor, its version and what was checked here.

## Errors and failure behaviour

`RecordError` (non-exhaustive): `UnknownCanvas`, `NoProgram`, `InvalidUrl`, `Bind`, `Problems`, `NotBound`, `Source { name, error }`, `UnsupportedSource`, `NoAdapter { port, tried }`, `Graph`, `Engine` (for example a partition id already in use), `Start(Vec<StartFailure>)`, `IsoRunning`, `Iso`. Run-time failures do not panic or end the process: they are `failure()` and `StartReport::sources[i].failed`; the nodes' own restart rules apply (a source with a recoverable error restarts with back-off, ADR-0024; an output that wrote media never restarts, ADR-0038).

## Performance requirements

Control-thread only; the graph's hot paths are the nodes' and keep their budgets. Five worker threads while recording (render, audio, two encoders, the output), three without audio. A second output adds one (its output) when it shares both encoders, up to three when it has its own. Separate audio files add two doorbell workers (their encoder and their output) and use none of those output slots; with no separate audio file open, those partitions have no worker. Separate video files add up to two cadence workers (their encoders) and share the output worker; image tracks add no worker beyond the output worker (they share it) and, per tick of an unchanged picture, one compare on the render thread; their exports and normalisation passes add GPU work to the render tick, which the `iso_cost` example measures. Every cadence partition gets the `Periodic` thread class from the engine's policy; the doorbell partitions get `Responsive`.

## Open questions

- After `end_output`, audio keeps flowing until the drain, so the file's audio may end a few frames (at most `END_TAIL_LIMIT` plus the caller's loop latency) after its last picture; trimming audio at the end time is not done (ADR-0045).
- A/V sync, measured on SC-PC 2026-11-03 (ADR-0042, Rejected): with Windows Media Player the system audio leads the picture by 26 to 30 ms (`ffplay` 34 ms; the 44 ms above included `ffplay`'s frame drops). Both capture timestamps are honest: a click test showed WASAPI loopback timestamps equal the device's play time (`IAudioClock`) within 0.2 ms, and the display's frame age at render ticks is 0.5 to 4 ms.
